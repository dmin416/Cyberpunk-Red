## Foreword

Thirty years ago, we at R. Talsorian set out on a mission. The idea was simple: take a dark-future city and dissect it, block by block, function by function, in a way that had never been done before. And we called it the *Night City Sourcebook*.

This project wasn't just going to be a list of buildings thrown down on a page; Night City would be the result of a long and evolved city-building process. Buildings would be placed with their own internal logic, then illustrated with 3D (actually orthographic) maps that were indexed to show not only what a place looked like, but the layout of surrounding buildings and even where the data terms and other utilities players would need to use were.

Remember; this was back in the dark ages of computer gaming – before 3D programs would make building a city in three dimensions something you could do with the press of a button. Each building needed to be built with draftsman's precision, and placed exactly where we wanted it, because once it was down on the page (and populated by the many many characters we created to inhabit that space) it wasn't going to be moved without a lot of pain and eraser marks. So Night City became a project that held realistic depth and realistic urban planning.

How realistic? When, in 2012, the Polish computer whizzes at CD Projekt Red asked us if they could borrow Night City and the world of Cyberpunk for a project they (as big fans of the roleplaying game) really wanted to do, we could confidently hand them a real city that not only was exciting and dark, but that also worked. And the developers at CDPR were amazingly faithful to that original vision; I'm astounded that I can still wander around the mean streets of the video game and see places I recognize even now, and that still look like they were taken right out of a brain feed dump of my thoughts. This exactitude continues, all the way to the recent *Edgerunners* anime, which is so true to the City that players often take their game characters on dates in places the anime's heroes went to on their own romantic liaisons.

So although years have passed since its first inception, Night City is still vibrant and alive; even after the 4th Corporate War and a nuke decimated the Corporate Center. It's got history and depth; you can still find the places we invented back then (the Afterlife anyone?) and even find places that have been reduced to legacies buried under the ruins of the post-nuke urban zones. It's all there, waiting for you to explore.

So now, we're going to hand you the keys to our city. This incarnation is a bit different from the 2020 version (after all there was a war a few years back) since it is too expansive to go block by block, but it stands as a waystation for what eventually will evolve into the Night City known to most veterans of the *Cyberpunk 2077* video game. We've positioned it in the time between the 4CW and the reclaimed era of the 2070s, as part of the overarching Cyberpunk chronology and a way to give you a taste of how this city evolves. Because this isn't the final Night City. It lives and breathes and builds on itself, just like a real city. And with this book, it's yours.

Welcome to Night City in the Time of the Red.

Awwwoooo!

Mike Pondsmith,  
and the Crew at R. Talsorian Games.
