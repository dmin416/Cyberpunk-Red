## Little Europe

It’s me, Ziggy "Front" Page (Media 7), frequent star contributor to Night City Today News! As an independent Media, I prefer to go where the work is (and where the eurobucks are), instead of doing glorified puff pieces in the hopes that some mouth-breathing suit sees my work and wants to offer me a name badge and dental insurance. I can’t buy food with exposure, but getting paid to do a field guide with Trace Santiago? While keeping total creative control over my copy? Count me in! Enough with that. Let’s talk Little Europe.
I once heard Little Europe described as a divided district composed of tight-knit neighborhoods, with crumbling brick buildings sitting side by side next to towering skyscrapers. Let me tell you, choomba: "divided" is right.
Little Europe’s divided between classes, between architectural styles, and between economic powers, as major corps snuggle up next to the blue-collar small businesses they look down on. It’s even divided between the past and the future, with the Night City spaceport construction site across the water to the west and the Hot Zone glowing in the east.
**Security Code 7:** NCPD, Hardened Medium | **Utility Code:** 3

And yet, despite the overall vibe of "trying too hard" that the place gives, I find myself coming back again and again. For such a divided district, everything feels connected. It’s a place where people live, not one where they just go to shop and party, trying to survive a suburban hell.
Everyone is invested in making Little Europe work, or at least in putting on a damn good show of it. Sure, everyone wants it to work in a different way, and everything feels like it’s permanently at 110% intensity, but no one is here because it’s trendy or because they have nowhere else to go. People from all over decide that Little Europe is where they want to be, swapping resources, culture, language, and punches. It's organic diversity, making Little Europe as comfortable as that spiked faux-leather jacket you’ve broken in.

### City Manager

In Little Europe, City Manager is an elected position, chosen by local property owners every four years. That generally means business owners - and in Little Europe, that means folks who are comfortable with both sides of the law. Yet elections tend to go pretty smoothly, and there’s rarely violence over the results. During the campaigning? That’s another story.
The current City Manager is Amanda Polishchuk (Solo 6). Despite earning a nickname of "The Pole" from clients who can’t read a map, Polishchuk is actually an Ukrainian-American who made her bones during the 4th Corporate War and retired from freelance Solo work in 2040. Seeing many of her former colleagues transition into business ownership, she decided to help them rebuild Little Europe by running for City Manager.
Many of those same colleagues went to bat for her during the election, giving her a combative edge on the competition. Now she’s ready to protect the district from other City Managers looking to poach resources, land, and people. Emilia Ortega of Downtown has personally earned Polishchuk’s ire.
Rumor has it that Polishchuk provides funding to the Undertow and encourages their violent "protest activities" in Downtown.

### Population

Following the 4th Corporate War, pieces and parts of several Night City districts were smushed together because of need, desire, and a little creative gerrymandering. Little Italy and Northside combined to form the core of Little Europe, and it still shows in the older architecture. There are also parts of old West Hill in the mix. Add the corporations that moved in, and you’ve got a pretty eclectic population of artists, mercs, suits, and blue-collar workers.
The majority of people in Little Europe tend to float around what our grandparents used to call "middle class" and what we now call "working class" (or, if you prefer, "not absolutely destitute"). Sure, the district holds the company headquarters of Night Corp and Danger Gal, and you’ll find the occasional high-end shop here and there, but if you took a random sample of citizens who actually live here, you’d end up with people who are just about able to cover their rent and only eat Kibble once a day.

### Security Provider

The security provider for Little Europe is the NCPD, and unlike in some other parts of Night City, they’ll respond to emergency calls here. They also patrol regularly and are known to look for excuses to stop and frisk folks to flex their muscles. In other words, this isn't the best place to walk around looking like a human armory.

### Recent History

Downtown broke off from Little Europe in 2042, when the Chamber of Commerce ponied up enough dough to pay for a seat on the Night City Council. There’s been a cold war ever since. The border between the two districts gets redrawn at least once a year as each side works to sway Council votes through negotiation, bribes, and occasional bursts of violence.
A few months ago, some large, gift-wrapped boxes left in a day market exploded, spreading clouds of chemically enhanced synthcoke across the area - transforming about a hundred ordinary citizens into addicts on the spot.

### Locations

#### #1 101.1 Killzone
**Security Code 6:** NCPD, Hardened Medium
Three years ago, someone pulled together the abandoned equipment from KJBR Studios to rebuild a new station.
Rumor is it was done by Rockaby Rozalyn (Rockerboy 5), one of the hottest DJs in the city once upon a time, but no one knows for sure. All they know is that the old KJBR building is - somehow - still standing, and now someone has used that equipment to snag 101.1 on the dial, pumping out hard rock as Killzone. The station plays a lot of rare singles and imported hits, so it’s gaining popularity fast.

#### #2 Biotechnica Reference Forest RF-14
**Security Code 10:** Biotechnica Security, Hardened Heavy
Jammed between the chrome of the rebuilding urban center and the polluted waters of Del Coronado Bay is a thin sliver of green, owned and operated by Biotechnica.
Reference Forest RF-14 is an experiment focused on genetically modifying Cypress and Eucalyptus trees to thrive in a heavily polluted urban environment.
RF-14 was open to the public as a park until last year, when a brawl between the Eurotrashers and the Undertow resulted in fire damage to several trees. Way to go, doombas. Today, Biotechnica security, backed up by a pack of cyberwolves, patrols the small forest to prevent further damage.

#### #4 Camden Court
**Security Code 8:** NCPD, Hardened Heavy
Once people think about putting down roots, they start demanding a safe, comfortable place to live. City Manager Amanda Polishchuk herself bought Camden Court when she retired from Solo work in 2040, then restored it to its pre-4CW glory, creating the most fortified and secure apartment building in the district. She also added just the right amount of ritz to let the neighbors know that this is a place where important people live. The amount of physical and NET security here is ridiculous. It’s able to hold off anything short of a decked-out corporate assault team. This makes it attractive not only to retired edgerunners who have the cash to spend on such amenities, but also to actively successful Solos and security-conscious corporate Execs who appreciate not having to compromise on safety or comfort.
The fact that the City Manager herself lives in the Penthouse means NCPD responds to emergencies promptly. Sometimes the perps are even alive by the time the cops arrive - every apartment seems to house its own armory. Even Maelstrom has gotten it through their titanium-reinforced skulls that Camden Court is off-limits.

#### #5 Chopper’s
**Security Code 5:** NCPD, Hardened Light
A lot of tourists - including the occasional ganger from other districts - think Chopper’s is a biker bar based on the name, but, in fact, it used to be the local butcher shop. Then the 4CW came, times got hard … and when Norman's walk-in freezer broke down, he decided to switch from slinging meat to slinging drinks. Norman is the owner, by the way: a baggy fellow who realized his old ice boxes could work double duty, as both alcohol storage and a makeshift bar to serve drinks on. It gives the whole place an intriguing DIY flair over the joint’s faint but-persistent odor of blood. The drinks are as unassuming as the decor, with a shot of rotgut or a glass of beer going for 10eb a pop.
Of course, that doesn’t mean Chopper’s is entirely out of the meat business. There’s a Fixer by the name of 80/20 (Fixer 3) who does business out of the back room. The old freezer may not work, but it turns out the meat grinder still does! If you’re looking for some preowned cyberware and are okay with not asking many questions about where it came from, 80/20 might be able to help you out. What you get will need cleaning, but you can’t beat the prices.
Members of the Eurotrashers hang out here since Kate Mulvaney won’t let them set foot in Fiddler’s Green.

#### #6 Continental Brands
Vertical Neighborhood

**Security Code 8:** Continental Brands Security, Hardened Light
The Continental Brands Vertical Neighborhood is sometimes called "Flavors of the World" because of the Continental Brands slogan hanging over the front door.
Continental Brands originally built it as exclusive housing for their employees, before the suits in charge realized they had more floor space than workers and opened it up to the occasional outsider.
The complex is notorious because it’s a nightmare to navigate. Each floor is laid out like a movie version of a different country’s old-world market, complete with winding streets, street vendor holograms, and even scent vents that pump out the aroma of non-existent street food.
The actual conapt doors are disguised to look like storefronts, making it all the more befuddling. Imagine living in "Ye Old Pie Shoppe" instead of "Apartment 5C."
The place is so confusing that some of the local kids hire themselves out as guides. Fast Eddie (Fixer 1) is the most reliable of the bunch, and his rates are the most reasonable (10eb for an ambush-free experience). Continental Brands refuses to change the building’s layout, but "got lost in a maze" is also not a valid excuse for showing up late to work.

#### #7 Cube-A-Rama
**Security Code 5:** NCPD, Hardened Light
Cubes are cubes are cubes. They’re glorified closets with delusions of livability. That said, if you have to live in a box with a bed for a floor, Cube-A-Rama isn’t a bad option.
The owners repurposed an old brick apartment building and sliced the units into uneven cubes. If you’re lucky, you might get an extra few centimeters! They even kept the original façade to give it an extra bit of pre-4CW charm.
A Netrunner collective that used to meet up at Short Circuit rents out most of one floor. No idea what their goals are. They could be anything from an anarchist commune dedicated to overthrowing the government to an indie game studio hoping to release the next Elflines Online heartbreaker. They aren’t fond of sharing and tend to speak in binary code.

#### #8 Danger Gal Housing Facility
**Security Code 10:** Danger Gal Security, Hardened Medium
Across the street from the Danger Gal Offices is the Danger Gal housing facility, often called the "Danger Zone" by people who desperately want to be creative.
It’s an Art Deco compound with a vague resemblance to Japanese schools from old anime, and it contains Danger Gal’s - let’s call it "distinctive" - flair for decoration. If you decide to live there, I hope you like pink because that’s what you’ll see inside, only occasionally broken up by the neon purple, pastel green, and ultraviolet, all enhanced by black light.
The Danger Zone embodies the "work hard, play hard" ethos of Danger Gal, with the residents hanging out around the endless retro arcade machines, beanbag pits, obstacle courses, and machines that dispense Kibble cookies and hot and cold running Smash. It’s not uncommon for Piranhas or the Eurotrashers to show up and hang out, but only residents are allowed into the gym, pool, and built-in firing range.

#### #9 Danger Gal Offices
**Security Code 10:** Danger Gal Security, Hardened Heavy
Yes, your favorite anime-obsessed detectives are stationed right here in Little Europe. Don’t be put off by the stately Art Deco building. Underneath that vintage clock tower, you’ll find thirteen floors of pink shag carpet, stuffed animals, painted model robots, and smiling cat girls who will gladly shoot you dead with their brightly-colored guns while five different J-pop songs play at the same time.
Don’t let the bubblegum otaku vibe fool you, though.
These detectives are tough, and they take their contracts and their clients seriously.
Only the first floor is open to the public, with offices for client meetings and an upscale maid café that serves drinks and snacks. Just be careful what you say while visiting. Rumor is that all those kawaii maids and dashingly

handsome butlers are actually operatives in disguise, collecting intel from besotted customers.
Danger Gal has, without a doubt, some of the best security in Little Europe, so think twice before starting trouble there. The company’s slogan of "little detective, big gun" is absolutely accurate on all counts. … and they say there’s no truth in advertising anymore.

#### #10 Everything and More
**Security Code 6:** NCPD, Hardened Medium
Before the 4th Corporate War, Everything and More was the premiere department store in this part of the city. Since than, it has downsized and is now a larger-than-average, upscale bodega, primarily serving the residents of Camden Court.

#### #11 Fiddler’s Green
**Security Code 9:** NCPD, Hardened Heavy
Fiddler’s Green is about as Irish as green beer, but you have to admit, the place is committed to the gimmick, even if Father Kevin and Dillon Murphy pop their heads in once in a while to complain about cultural appropriation.
During lunch hours, Fiddler’s Green serves corned beef SCOP on cabbage SCOP alongside stew made from stout, and on the third Monday of the month they even have real soda bread, somehow.
Their house beer, the Pint O’ Plain (20eb a pint), is an actual imported stout from Ireland, and the nomads that bring shipments in don’t shake the kegs too badly. It’s a lively place favored by NCPD officers and war vets, but they’re more likely to stop fights than start them, so it feels more like a family pub instead of a place for gangs to rumble.
The owner/bartender is Kate Mulvaney (Solo 6), a vet of the SouthAm wars who got an honorable discharge and a medical-grade cyberarm for her service.
She runs the place with her wife, Audrey, and the rest of their family. They’re no strangers to the local edgerunners, who hang out here for a pint and a bit of gossip about new jobs floating around Little Europe. Kate even acts as an unofficial post office, holding packages and messages for regular customers seeking to communicate with clients and colleagues without revealing their home address.

#### #12 Greta’s
**Security Code 5:** NCPD, Hardened Light
Greta’s is a dive bar, but it’s a special kind of dive bar.
About a million years ago, it catered exclusively to lesbians, but after the 4CW, every small business had to evolve or die, so it transformed into a pool hall. In fact, over the past few years, it’s become the pool hall of Night City. Technically, the best players here aren’t pro since they don’t pull a salary, but they make money off of bets.
Greta’s, meanwhile, pulls in plenty of eb from the drinks sold to the audience. The Sunk Pocket is a particular favorite: only 20eb gets you a shot of cherry-infused vodka and cognac, topped with whipped cream and a cherry. It shows that Greta’s lesbian clientele is still loud and proud at the bar.
If you don’t understand how I got from a cherry drink to here, get your friendly neighborhood sapphic to explain it to you.

#### #13 Holy Angels Church
**Security Code 8:** 6th Street, Hardened Light
Most people don’t think of religion when they think of Night City, but we have our houses of worship, too. One of the most venerable is Holy Angels Church. The threestory stone structure is built in faux-Gothic style and is frequented by Catholics and anyone else desperate enough to turn to the Church for sanctuary or spiritual guidance.
Holy Angels is run by Father Kevin (Solo 7), a seventy-five-year-old Irishman who still looks spry for his age.
Although Father Kevin is the head priest, most day-to-day operations fall to Father Paul (Rockerboy 4), a Nigerian transplant who takes care of the place with his wife, Lucy (Exec 3). They're especially kind towards young women who remind them of the daughters they lost during the 4th Corporate War.

The Fathers don’t allow weapons or violence inside the church, and they aren’t afraid to invoke the sanctuary of the Church in front of the NCPD, making it a good place for gangers to meet and talk biz. 6th Street has stationed a security contingent just outside the Church, gratis, to guard it. They claim it is a community service, but more cynical minds see it as a publicity stunt to drum up more security business.

#### #15 Night Corp HQ
**Security Code 10:** Lazarus, Hardened Heavy ◊ Utility Code: 2
The self-styled legacy of city founder Richard Night, Night Corp has had a few names over the decades but has settled on its current one in recent years. It’s the glue that holds the city together. It was Night Corp that negotiated the creation of the current City Council.
Night Corp is a land developer and contractor with a wide variety of public works in its portfolio, which means they’re involved in everything from construction jobs on

brand-new buildings to dumping the trash from the old ones. They even pay people, in cash and free cyberware, to clean up the Hot Zone, all in the name of keeping the city moving forward rather than disintegrating into warfare. Of course, that’s all from the company’s press pack, but there’s probably a grain of truth in there somewhere.
In fact, no one knows much about what Night Corp is really up to or where they get their seemingly endless supply of eurobucks. Of course, as an ethical journalist, I would never publish baseless allegations about a major civic-minded corporation in a travel guide such as this. I certainly would never imply that all of that free cyberware Night Corp gives out to people as thanks for cleaning things up has secret biomonitors built in to scrape medical data. And I definitely would never suggest that the company preys on desperate people in order to experiment on them. Obviously, those are just unfounded rumors circulating the city, and I only bring them up so you can know what kinds of lies to look out for.

#### #16 Old Saint Christopher Center
**Security Code 8:** NCPD, Hardened Medium
Little Europe isn’t all dive bars, retired Solos in their autumn years, and hyperactive cat girl detectives. Even after the separation from Downtown, little bits of high-class culture sometimes take root here. The Old Saint Christopher Center is a perfect example. The ancient building is a five-star hotel with an attached convention center for corporate shindigs, decorated to the nines inside but still wearing its old, crumbling façade like a carnival mask. The lobby is grandiose, with a swanky bar and boutiques that’ll make your wallet cry just from looking at them. Word is, there’s a casino tucked away in the basement, reserved for the hotel’s most exclusive guests. You won’t find that in the brochure, though.

#### #17 Paradiso Terrestre
**Security Code 8:** NCPD, Hardened Medium
Superstar chef Leonardo Calvano (Rockerboy 7) opened his exclusive restaurant, Paradiso Terrestre, earlier this year. Since I’m more likely to get elected City Manager of the Exec Zone than to score a reservation to Paradiso Terrestre, I can’t speak to the quality of the food - which is listed as "Southern Neo-Italian," whatever the hell that means - but my colleague Jericho Hunt did get to interview Calvano for Night City Today News, so I can give you the short version based on his write-up.
Paradiso isn’t so much a restaurant as a private dining experience. You’re picked up by an armed and armored luxury AV, complete with an onboard bartender - sorry, a "mixologist" - who makes you a custom drink as you ride to the restaurant. You and your group will be the only people there, and Calvano personally cooks and serves the several-course meal. When you’re done, the aerodyne drops you off wherever your party wishes to go next. The experience is exclusive, intimate, and insanely expensive.
The dishwasher there, who only goes by the name Polly (Fixer 3), has been known to leak the odd bit of gossip or the establishment’s reservation schedule if you slip them more eurobucks than Calvano pays - which isn’t much.

#### #18 Red Cab Company
**Security Code 6:** Maelstrom, Hardened Light
Everyone has their favorite bit of Night City to point at when they want to say, "This is Night City in a nutshell," or "only Night City could make something like this." For me, it’s Red Cab. If you’ve spent more than a few minutes outside the Combat Zones, odds are you’ve seen these distinctively red groundcars all over the streets. They’re well-maintained, well-armored, and easily summoned with an Agent or a data term. Red Cab is the largest private transport company in the city, and it loves to cloak its capitalism in a glaring Neo-Soviet aesthetic. Leave it to Night City to find a way to turn communism into a capitalist gold mine.
The main dispatch office and garage are situated in Little Europe. They’re based in the rougher part of the district, although I don’t know whether that’s because the lease was cheap or because their competitors snatched up all the good plots of land. Regardless, they have enough room for their groundcars and a small fleet of AVs, as well as a small target range so the cabbies can get in some pistol practice between shifts.
Maelstrom provides security at Red Cab. Rumor has it that the company "ships" Maelstrom’s goods around the city for free in exchange for the service.

#### #19 Revere Courier Services
**Security Code 5:** NCPD, Hardened Light
Originally based in City Center before its move to Little Europe, Revere Courier Services is a staple of Night City and the closest thing the place has to a functioning mail service. The company makes good cash on emergency deliveries and secure physical data transfers for companies too paranoid to trust the CitiNet. They pay edgerunners and other freelance security professionals promptly and generously to protect high-priority shipments. The rest of the time, Revere does normal, mundane shipping, transporting cargo around, into, and out of Night City on the daily.
Since Revere set up shop in the district, a few of the old-school residents have signed up for a monthly subscription service to send and receive all their regular packages. Most edgerunners still prefer dead drops or handoffs at places like Fiddler’s Green for hot items and messages, though.

#### #20 Shady Oaks Elementary
**Security Code 4:** NCPD, Hardened Light
When you settle down and start a family somewhere, that often means children. Those kids have to be educated somewhere, even in Night City. A large percentage of Night City kids get their education from holos and braindances sold by WNS, Segotari, and Net54, but families with money can send their kids to an old-fashioned physical school like Shady Oaks Elementary.
And "shady" is the right word! The school’s Garden Patch boasts a "bespoke, in-person education custom-tailored to each child’s needs." What really happens is that the kids are dropped off, walked through the doors by smiling adults, and then plugged into custom-designed braindance units for hours.

Yes, Short Circuit can be reduced down to a bullet point that reads, "Tech and Netrunner bar," and yes, 20eb will get you the house drink, the Code Freeze - vodka and the Triti-Fizz flavor du jour on ice. ## Upper Marina

The district began with a vision, shared by most of the residents with money, of an Upper Marina that recaptured the pre-4CW glory days. And honestly, on the surface?
It seems well on its way. New business ventures spring up as fast as the buildings to house them, the NCART stations are normally pumped free of water, and there are jobs to be had in research, services, and manual labor.
Behind the hope, though, lies a grim reality; investors and absentee owners with deep pockets have moved into pieds-à-terre, but the locals servicing their needs are seeing few benefits. Our sources report cut corners on construction projects and predatory behavior against the independent businesses struggling to make a go of things here.
There’s an atmosphere of haste and superficial commitment. The art scene is ostensibly thriving, but those artists are producing with an eye toward commercial success so they can move on to better and more lucrative things.
The insincerity extends to the businesses; no matter how "artisanal" your creations are, there are only so many pop-up boba-tea-SCOP-slop-shops that one area can support, and many of the owners are cynics cosplaying at being artsy and bohemian.

### City Manager

Kabria Chung (Rockerboy 5), elected three years ago to a six-year term by the district’s property owners, wants the Upper Marina that Richard Night wrote about: breezy, bohemian neighborhoods full of art, entertainment, and gorgeous boats for the city to admire. She wants quaint houseboats, ornately decorated skyscrapers, and outdoor malls with wrought-iron-framed glass roofs. Her staff, on the other hand, wants kickbacks. Chung’s office approves quick-build projects that start rotting as soon as you scratch the paint. Then, when buildings collapse or their developers skip town, her staffers "regretfully" sell the land for redevelopment (and pocket a "commission"). Whether Chung is blind to the graft and corruption or a willing participant depends on who you ask.

### Population

The population of the Upper Marina is stratified. At the top, you’ve got the yacht-owning rich who dine in high-end restaurants and relax in expensive spas. At the bottom are the workaday residents who support them.
They fill the service and construction industries that form the bedrock of the community, but most still struggle to make rent. There’s also underworld activity, with the Skiv Family edging in from across the canal and Maelstrom holding firm to territory on the western edge of the district.

### Security Provider

NCPD provides security here, with a lighter touch than in less salubrious areas.

### City Manager

Chung encourages the local cops to behave as she feels police "should" rather than as a gang with a better grade of uniform.
Appearances are everything. Higher-class establishments and inhabitants get a lot of leeway, but doors still get kicked down in the lower-rent apartments.

### Recent History

If you live in the Upper Marina, be ready to talk sports, business, and the arts. In baseball, the Slammers are on track to win the West Coast Series. In basketball, the Heat lost their shot at the playoffs by one game. I’ve also heard rumors that the Nuke just swiped a star murderball player from Vancouver’s roster. If so, that might put the team in line for a championship next year.
When it comes to business, Ziggurat, established in 2030, remains the neocorp giant towering over the district. The company’s CEO, UR, recently held a motivational seminar that sold out McCartney Field Stadium.
Hydrosubsidium has quietly expanded its R&D enterprise in the warehouses on the waterfront, allowing it to transport people and equipment without fuss. Meanwhile, a pair of towers earmarked for residential and retail use had to be demolished before they even opened due to overly-aerated concrete that started to dissolve after a single shower of blood rain. The land has been bought up by a speculator with plans to rebuild, but currently stands empty.
If you’re interested in the arts, you’ll want to check out Lola Lola’s upcoming art show at GraffitiX. Rumor has it, the total value of the pieces on display will exceed fifty million. There’s also the music scene. If you know the right Fixer, you might still be able to pick up tickets to the upcoming Kira <3 concert at the Stadium.

## Downtown

### City Manager

Three years ago, the Chamber of Commerce bought itself a seat on the Night City Council and carved territory out of Little Europe to create Downtown. The Chamber unilaterally appointed Emilia Ortega (Exec 6) to oversee day-to-day operations as City Manager. Ortega’s tough. Smart. And she used to captain one of those giant cargo ships back before the 4CW, so she knows how to administrate.
I’ll give her this: she doesn’t just try to steamroll over the people who live here. Instead, Ortega seems content to let nature take its course. Let the old businesses die, then arrange a buyout so that ugly skyscrapers can rise in their place.
There’s a limit to her power, though. No matter what Ortega says or does, she only has so much power. Mister Kernaghan’s the real force behind everything. He controls the Chamber of Commerce and most of the district’s supply lines.

### Population

The people make Downtown what it is. You’ve got the old families who’ve been here since the nuke. They mostly run small shops, vintage boutiques, and repair joints. They’re trying to hold onto their traditions, resisting the corporate takeover. Then there’s the new crowd - entrepreneurs, opportunists, and cronies of the Chamber. They’re opening cyberware clinics, designer gear stores, and luxury bodysculpt shops, trying to turn the district into a hotspot for Night City’s elite.

### Security Provider

Whitewater Security, the City Manager’s private security force, keeps the so-called "order." The upper ranks are filled by people who served under Ortega back in her ocean-going days. The street soldiers are mostly locals who buy into the idea that a gentrified Downtown is Night City’s future. Whitewater patrols the streets, but they’re not here for us. They’re here to protect the Chamber’s interests.
They watch over the businesses that pay their dues and they crack down on anyone who doesn’t. Some folks feel safer with Whitewater around, but in my opinion, they’re no different than any other gang - just better equipped.

### Recent History

Most of Downtown used to be this fancy district named West Hill Gardens. But when the 4CW hit, the rich and powerful fled and the working class moved in. After the new Night City Council formed, it looped us together with a few other districts to create Little Europe. But the way we saw it, the rest of the district didn’t matter. We were Main Street. Downtown. The beating heart of a new Night City.
About five years ago, things started going south.
Supplies dried up. Gangs rolled in. Infrastructure crumbled. Looking back, I’m realizing this was all part of the plan. The Chamber of Commerce gave us the squeeze before buying a seat on the Night City Council, probably bankrolled by Continental Brands. It ain’t a coincidence that Continental Plaza broke ground the day after Emilia Ortega planted her butt in the City Manager’s chair.
Since then, the biggest noise has come from the criminal underworld. Not a day goes by that the Undertow isn’t causing trouble, usually with low-grade explosives and gunfire. Last week, the Eastern Tigers Triad and Whitewater had a massive shootout down by the waterfront. Rumor has it that the Triad hijacked one of Mister Kernaghan’s shipments from Asia. The Skiv family, meanwhile, has been hitting the Chamber on another front by sabotaging construction sites run by crews without mob ties.

## The Hot Zone

Despite twenty years of reconstruction, you’ll still find block after block of toppled buildings and wreckage here - hundreds of ruins full of the scraps of lost lives and lost dreams, all coated in lung-eating cancer dust.
Feels like a real-life braindance adventure, doesn’t it?
Thinking like that is how you die. I came here cocky, but this place shook me. Elsewhere in Night City, people are the threat, not the landscape. Sure, Hot Zone inhabitants will shoot you, but this place is the real enemy. Radiation.
Toxic material. Unstable skyscrapers. Diamond-carbon debris. Sinkholes. Forgotten power cables. Sheet glass falling from tilted buildings. Even the experienced nomad construction crews rebuilding around the edges of the Hot Zone lose people.
Deadly as it is, hundreds, maybe thousands, of people still exist here. Last-chance refugees hide in basements.
Scavvers dig for salvage. Thrillseekers and dorphers chase highs. And the corporations do what corporations always do: seize whatever they want, no matter the legality or cost.
Stuff happens in the Zone that cracks even Night Citybred chill. Street life gets desperate, but this is beyond that. People trapped here need rescue. Or killing. Or, sometimes both.
Nobody deserves the Zone.

### City Manager

There’s no "city" here to manage. Officially, the Night City Council collectively oversees the area, sure. This used to be the West Coast’s priciest real estate, after all, and nobody wants to give up their piece. They also don’t want to pay for cleanup. So Night Corp foots most of the bill, and nomads perform back-breaking work to clear it, meter by meter, year by year. The city begrudgingly assigns security details to stop folks from shooting the work crews, but they don’t go any farther in than they need to.

### Population

Nobody lives in the Hot Zone by choice. Both corp-sponsored and privately run salvage squads dig here, but they don’t live here. Everyone who lands here does so because no other place will take them. They’re out of money, out of friends, out of favors - people nobody will hire to haul cargo or sweep up on the Heywood Docks. Streetrats on the run from the gangs. Desperate souls rejected by every other community. They do whatever keeps them alive: scavving, begging, stealing, running drugs, and the worst kinds of body trades.
I take that back. Almost everyone. There are also gangers, criminals, and outright monsters who like it here.
And the nasty part is, the longer you live in the Zone, the easier it is to slip from that first group into the second.

### Security Provider

"Where are the cops?" I hear you ask. The security drones that patrol the Zone’s edges aren’t hard to avoid, and inside the Zone? There just aren’t any.
While NCPD has jurisdiction here, you won’t see them on the ground. Officers fly over the Zone in armored AVs with door-mounted sniper rifles, picking off anyone they see firing a gun. They dump gas grenades on scavver turf battles that threaten to spill over into the "real" city. When they bother to respond, they respond hard.
I have it on good authority that some lowlife in Charter Hill offers AV tours where rich bastards can snipe at or drop barrel bombs on squatters who’re just trying to stay alive in the wreckage. If you’re going to the Zone, gear up and bring a fire team to increase your odds of survival.

### Recent History

The Hot Zone shrinks a little more each year as crews rebuild their way in from the edges of The Glen, Little Europe, and the Upper Marina. Not everyone is happy about it. Maelstrom frequently sabotages reclamation sites, and NCPD has caught scavver groups and Reckoners doing the same.
Zhirafa’s had problems with their newest heavy equipment, which could be sabotage, too, or maybe just the result of metal fatigue caused by the brutal conditions. Maelstrom and the Reckoners are clashing over Reckoner "recruitment" in squatter camps, but Maelstrom beefs with everyone, so it probably won’t turn into a larger war.

## Little China

### City Manager

The Little China Redevelopment Association (LCRA) was established even before the first blood rain dried on the street in the 2020s, ostensibly to guide the rebuilding of Little China in "a manner harmonious with its residents." That’s bullshit. It exists to rubber-stamp whatever David Ling Po (Fixer 8) wants. Mr. Ling Po is inextricably associated with Little China and its fortunes. He sculpts the district like a favorite bonsai, his to mold and shape... or ruthlessly prune.
A Stanford graduate with a Harvard MBA, Mr. Ling Po appears to be an illustrious businessman and philanthropist, but he owns Little China from sewers to penthouses.
Does someone sell steamed buns? Ling Po takes a cut from the sale, and probably sold the vendor the SCOP filling, the Kibble dough, and collects rent on their steamer cart, besides. Here are a few of the "rumors" circulating around him.
◊ Ling Po heads the Weng Fang Tong. If so, that means
that he controls, or at least profits from, most of the sex work and gambling in Little China and Night City. This one is true.
◊ Ling Po has had women kidnapped, resculpted, and
reprogrammed as sex workers. This seems like Yellow Peril hooey to me. It’s simpler to buy spare children from Rancho Coronado or starving Reclaimer communities just outside of Night City.
◊ Ling Po installs broadcast-capable cybereyes in some
(or all) of his hirelings, transforming them into living security cameras. I’m not sure if it's true, but it is a terrifying thought.
◊ David Ling Po sends every child in Little China a book
voucher hóngbāo each Lunar New Year. This is true and goes a long way towards buying goodwill and loyalty among Little China’s residents. By the way, I bought trashy manhua with mine.

### Population

The center and north of Little China’s population is roughly fifty percent ethnic Chinese, thirty-five percent Asian of other ancestry, and fifteen percent "other." David Ling Po has spent filthy amounts of lucre propping up the local Chinese population at the expense of other groups. The LCRA bursaries and rebuilding vouchers require a background check, and the only people who pass the checks happen to be Chinese. This has led to resentment against Chinese residents, who then ask for more protection. Mr.
Ling Po sends his Gold Dragon "protectors" after popular scapegoats. This has allowed him to selectively depopulate parts of Little China that were once parts of Studio City and the old Charter Hill.
The south of Little China is mostly outside of the LCRA’s control, and it shows. The Red Chrome Legion holds a disturbing amount of sway there, recruiting from those pushed out of north and central Little China by redevelopment activity. Clashes between the Legion and the Gold Dragons along the border are common.

### Security Provider

The Gold Dragons were a generic boostergang until the LCRA contracted them to provide security for Little China.
While they made a decent effort at the beginning, their impulsive tendencies left several of Mr. Ling Po’s pet shopkeepers battered despite the payment of protection money. The gang leaders responsible vanished, and new, better-trained members took up the vacant posts. At the top is Edwina "Eddy" Sing (Solo 4/Exec 2 ). She grew up on pirated kung-fu movies and has introduced a rudimentary, if simplistic and stereotypical, code of honor using martial arts duels to settle disputes. This means residents get some safety in exchange for their protection money as long as they don’t mind members of the Dragons jumping around on their cyberlegs like they’re wearing wire harnesses.
NCPD occasionally drives in from the Upper Marina, flash their lights for show, and then drives out again. The city cops never actually do anything.

### Recent History

Little China wasn’t exactly the classiest neighborhood to live in before the 4CW. Former residents of the old Charter Hill and Studio City, therefore, saw it as an insult when parts of those neighborhoods were forcibly annexed into Little China after Mr. Ling Po bought his council seat. Unsurprisingly, those who could afford to run moved to other districts.

*District map: see PDF p.98.*

David Ling Po does take care of his personal fief. He has made quite a bit of funding available to the LCRA - with strings attached, of course - and has fed and clothed destitute residents out of his personal warehouses (as long as they’re suitably useful). A brisk, busy air has displaced the cozy seediness of north and central Little China, as residents go about rebuilding their lives in half-fractured buildings stabilized using mutant bamboo scaffolding and Petrochem resin.
The reopening of the Guăngbō Tower last year was celebrated with fireworks and a lion dance troupe. Mr.
Ling Po even provided multiple roast pigs for the opening ceremony. Most of the residents I spoke to were more proud than annoyed that the LCRA prioritized the tower over other infrastructure. They feel it is a testament to their hard work and proof that Little China is recovering.

## University District

### City Manager

The man in charge of both the University District and Night City University itself is Dr. Edward Michaels, PhD (Exec 4/Fixer 6/Tech 4 ). He’s been Dean of NCU for decades and worked his ass off to protect it from the fallout of the 4th Corporate War by calling in every favor owed to him to transform the University from a simple school into a walled fortress of education. He used the university’s endowment to purchase the City Manager’s seat on the Night City Council and kept the lights on - and the students and faculty safe - even when the rest of the city fell into chaos. Sadly, even his legendary web of connections had to fall apart eventually, which explains why Dr. Michaels made a deal with Biotechnica.
In exchange for an infusion of cash, resources, and two out of five seats on the University’s board of trustees, Dr. Michaels gave Biotechnica permission to build their regional headquarters in the University District. Did Dr.
Michaels sell out NCU’s long-term future for short-term benefits? Or did he do the only thing he could to keep the University’s doors open? Only time will tell.

### Population

Stroll through the district, and you’ll see Night City University, a central buildingscape at the core of the area.
Students walk around at all hours, seemingly oblivious to the pressures of the outside world. Businesses line the streets outside the campus walls, allowing students and faculty to live their lives without ever leaving the safe bubble of the university’s protection.
Further away from campus, brown-bricked houses provide sanctuary for professors and the occasional graduate student. Some non-faculty staff members and employees of supporting businesses live in the district, but most commute in from other parts of the city. The university NCART stop sees heavy use.
On the east side of the district, Biotechnica reigns supreme. The majority of people found there are well educated, and many project a soft mad-scientist vibe - even the Execs. Biotechnica has a habit of promoting people with science and engineering degrees into management positions, not business majors.While a variety of gangers spend time in the District, including members of the Piranhas (to run the parties and sell drugs to students), the Dirty Hippies (also to sell drugs to students), the Voodoo Boys (yep, also drugs), and the Kill Krashers (they're just here for the violence), the two biggest gangs calling the area home are the Philharmonic Vampyres and the Princesses of Justice.

### Security Provider

Security for the district is provided by "the Maxes," a nickname for NCU Campus Security used by both students and faculty. Generally, campus Security isn’t as interested in making arrests as much as they are in keeping things quiet.
So long as things aren’t blowing up, people aren’t dying in the streets, and university business continues unmolested, Campus Security is happy to look the other way.

### City Manager

Zohara Freeman (Exec 7/Solo 2 ), the daughter of former Mayor Mbole Ebunike and famous actress Rachel Becher, was appointed City Manager last year by the Night Corp board of directors. This came out of nowhere.
Zohara, previously CEO of Combat Cabb, hadn’t shown any interest in politics. Rumor has it Zohara and her mother were seen walking into Night Corp’s HQ in Little Europe a few weeks before the announcement, but the rumors say fuck all about what was said inside.
Zohara is brilliant, charismatic, and analytical; a deadly trio for anyone standing in her way. She deftly threads her way through corporate demands and public needs and deals with anyone whose voice matters. A year into the office, she’s avoided a shitstorm of fallout from every disaster thrown her way - she even somehow placated the drivers outraged about the lack of guardrails on Embassy Street! She makes a point of being seen and spends her time out among the people of The Glen when not working in the office, showing up and shaking hands to prove that she lives here, just one of the masses. Of course, she’s doing so while being shadowed by bodyguards and a drone to record all her positive face time with the people, so make of it what you will.

### Population

They say Night City’s a multicultural metropolis, and that’s more valid in The Glen than anywhere else. Bop down the street and you’re likely to overhear conversations in a dozen languages. That’s especially true near the Consulate Causeway.
Thanks to resources poured into the district by Night Corp and other corporations, the majority of the population are corpzoners or government-employed equivalents. No ebs? Best skip The Glen, then. There are some cheap joints. I’ll drop them later, but on the whole, you gotta make steady bread to live here. This district was custom-designed to be just above the middle class for the working stiff, to push people into busting their butts to earn more scratch.
If you spend enough time here, you’ll end up eating lunch next to some fat cat or another, chatting with politicians from another country, or spotting some celebrity rushing along down the street. It’s not all fresh food and sunshine, though. If you know where to look, you’ll find the violent side of The Glen. I’m not talking about the Kill Krashers or the Reckoners, either. The Glen may be where laws are written for the common citizen, but people with power make their own laws. Cross one of them, and you’ll find out just how many bones they can order broken without paying the price. Spoiler alert: It’s all of them!

### Security Provider

The Glen is one of the best-protected pieces of turf in Night City, hands down. Only the Exec Zone sees tighter security. Half a dozen nameless mom-and-pop security companies operate out of the district with small jobs, but we all know it’s NCPD first, and for important buildings, Lazarus or Militech backing them up with private contracts. In a lot of Night City, NCPD might as well not exist, but here, where they have a near-instant backup and guns longer than I am tall, they swan around like they’re bulletproof. Folks like us do the dance whenever we’re harassed. "Yes, officer," and "no, officer," and of course, "whatever you need, officer," or risk getting beat down for being "uppity" or vanishing into the bowels of the Hall of Justice, never to be seen again.

## Old Japantown

### City Manager

When the Japanese corporation Sanroo decided to expand its operations to the Pacifica Confederation, it purchased cheap land in Old Japantown. Disliking the idea of being told "no" about any matter, the company chose to buy the local government outright rather than simply bribe it from the shadows.
The ever fashionable Adriane Casselle (Exec 7) serves double duty as the Branch Manager of Sanroo’s Pacifica Confederation operations and as City Manager for Old Japantown. A transplant from the Paris branch, Casselle knows strength comes from both making and subverting strong alliances. To that end, she has allied Sanroo with the Tyger Claws while also working with her lover, gang lieutenant Ryuji Shimazaki (Lawman 3), to slowly and quietly build a powerful spinoff organization loyal to her first and foremost.

### Population

While the core population of Old Japantown is still composed of people of Japanese descent, the district's demographics have diversified considerably since the 4th Corporate War, primarily due to the various gangs infesting the area. Maelstrom hovers on the edge of the district, and its forces raid Tyger Claw territory regularly before retreating back into the Hot Zone. The Iron Sights have been fighting the Red Chrome Legion for years. The war began in the southern part of Old Japantown and has pushed northward as the Iron Sights have forced the Red Chrome Legion further back with each defeat. Add to the gangers an eclectic mix of new residents who work in The Glen but can’t afford to live there, and there’s a little less "Japan" in Old Japantown every day.

### Security Provider

As difficult as it has been for them to hang onto the reins of the district, the Kimen-Gumi try to maintain their definition of law and order. Donning unique, colorful masks as their badge of office, the Kimen-Gumi serve as the public face of the Tyger Claws and have been deputized by City Manager Casselle to provide law enforcement in the district. Old Japantown isn’t as important in the Tyger Claws’ eyes as Kabuki, so the Kimen-Gumi aren’t as well-staffed as locals would like.

### Recent History

The rise of Kabuki and Watson diminished Old Japantown almost as much as the nuke. While funds from Sanroo help prop up the district, the Tyger Claws aren’t willing to commit the forces needed to stop the gang warfare. Everyone in the Kimen-Gumi knows an assignment to Old Japantown is either a punishment or a dead end in their career. The resentment this breeds has allowed Casselle and her lover Shimazaki to slowly subvert the loyalty of the troops.
The war between the Iron Sights and the Red Chrome Legion is an almost nightly affair. Last month alone, at least a dozen Old Japantown residents were killed or wounded in the crossfire. Rumor has it Casselle is considering negotiating with the Iron Sights sponsor, Hornet (Fixer 9/Medtech 4/ Tech 4 ), to provide support so the gang can drive the Legion fully across the border into Little China and out of the district for good. However, doing so might mean handing over more resources than Casselle is willing to give up. She knows Hornet is a man looking towards the future. He won’t be satisfied with payment of a few bribes and a bit of territory.
The icing on the Combat Zone cake for Old Japantown is a rise in cancer rates. Radioactive winds still occasionally blow in from the Hot Zone to the northwest. Experts insist the radiation levels should have long since died down, implying there must be an active source pumping out poison somewhere in the rubble.

## South Night City

### City Manager

Corruption starts at the top and flows downhill. For the people of South Night City, the source of all misery is Garven Haakensen (Fixer 7). Back during the 4th Corporate War, Haakensen sat in the mayor’s chair for a hot five minutes before the nuke exploded and everything went to hell.
Haakensen ran for the hills, fleeing like a coward, then slunk back in again ten years later with a trunk full of cash and a closet full of people’s secrets. That was enough to buy him the City Manager position in South Night City.
Haakensen considers himself a deal maker. He’s vain enough that he’s compared himself to Machiavelli, and the truth is, yeah, he does broker deals between rival factions on the City Council. Things do get done because he steps in. But he’s not nearly as slick as he thinks he is.
Otherwise, he wouldn’t have lost chunks of his territory like the Port of Night City in 2036 and the Reclamation Zone in 2039. Those losses obviously sting to this day, since Haakensen still rants about it in opinion pieces posted on the district’s official Garden Patch.
There isn’t any reason why South Night City shouldn’t be as prosperous and safe as its neighbors. The problem is that Garven Haakensen always eats his fill first, then scatters the crumbs to the people below him. Yes, every other City Manager likely does the same, but none to the same corrupt degree as South Night City’s boss of bosses.

### Population

South Night City is home to anyone down on their luck.
The population of this district is diverse and broad, with one commonality: the system either pushed them there or kept them there. Corpzoners would say we’re streetrats and criminals … and sure, some of us are. But everyone deserves to be treated like a human being. Well, almost everyone. Even I won’t fight for the rights of a Bozo.

### Security Provider

That’s a laugh! No one really provides security in South Night City except for the people who live there. The NCPD is afraid to step too far into the district unless they’re safe in armored vehicles, so groups like the DeadWoods and the Zoners have to look out for the people when the Kill Krashers or other gangs ride chaotically through the district.Okay, sure, each year Haakensen deputizes whichever company gives him the biggest kickback. Last year, it was Militech. This year, it’s a tiny little outfit named Scythe Security. Honestly, I’m baffled. Until last year, Scythe’s biggest contract was guarding Container Mart. Now, they’re deputized to enforce the law in the entire district?
Scythe doesn’t have the personnel, it doesn’t have the firepower, and it doesn’t have the money it would take to make Haakensen look in its direction, much less give them the nod. Something’s hinky. Scythe has been recruiting hard in the last few months, but right now, they don’t have the resources to do more than patrol property designated

### Recent History

The Zoners, a community activism group advocating for more funding to build up South Night City, marched on The Glen not long ago. The whole point of the protest was to shine a light on the injustice caused by funneling so much money into The Glen while ignoring South Night City. It didn’t go well.
NCPD showed up in force, and in the resulting chaos, people died. Cops. Zoners. It was a mess. The Zoners retreated and are currently planning their next steps.
One good thing did come out of the protest, though. The Glen’s City Manager, Zohara Freeman, decided to show up Haakensen by providing charity to SNC’s residents.
She’s paid for shipments of food, clean water, and bottled air to be distributed at key points throughout South Night City. Sure, it's a publicity stunt designed to make Freeman look good, and Haakensen look bad - but the folks who got a little more to eat and breathe aren’t complaining.
I mentioned how odd it was that Scythe Security got the district’s law enforcement contract earlier. As I noted, it makes no sense, even for a cheapskate like Haakensen, and I don’t have more than a set of disparate facts to help explain it. One, City Managers actually have to follow some of the rules imposed on them by the Council, including making a "good faith" effort to meet the basic needs of their constituents. Two, as Scythe fails at its job, the property values in South Night City are crashing down into the sewers. Three, a group of different fly-by-night companies have begun buying up those properties like flash paper at a fire sale.

## Port of Night City

### City Manager

Born Angela Ng (Nomad 10), we call the City Manager and Harbormistress "Calypso" because she’s a storm in human form. She’s a fleet captain, which ranks her just below the Council of Admirals that govern the Thelas. Outwardly, Calypso’s persona resembles that of an old romance movie pirate queen, but that’s just an affectation. Underneath the charming smile and clever banter, she has a brilliant mind for both logistics and tactics. For example, last year her leadership was pivotal in averting a crisis when armed Decker, Tanaka & Rogers ships tried to force their way into the harbor without paying. She fought those bastards into submission, then accepted their fees with a hearty "no harm done" after they surrendered.

### Population

The Port's population is mostly working-class. Of course, the nature of that work varies drastically depending on where in the Port you happen to be. The Thelas working near the sea are here on rotation. Most are young adults and haven’t yet started families. The land-side population is a mix of longtime Night City residents and recent Russian transplants looking for work.
Members of other nomad nations play their part here, too. The Snakes keep the railyards functioning, while the Aldecaldos and Jodes oversee the transfer of cargo to land-based transportation. Out on Flotsam, the population turns over on the regular. It's something of a retirement home for older Thelas, but it also serves as a port in a storm for younger nomads looking to blow off steam during shore leave.

### Security Provider

The Port of Night City is protected on two fronts. On the sea, Thelas gunships run harbor patrol, protecting not just the Port but the entire Night City coastline. On land, Thelas Marines keep order and respond to any security breaches. We’re a rough-and-tumble people, so the Marines usually don’t step in when a brawl breaks out.
They’re more interested in keeping the people safe and the Port secure than in butting in on your quarrel. Don’t let the seemingly small number of Marines present in the Port fool you, though. When trouble breaks out, every member of the Thelas nation is obligated to put down their cargo and pick up a weapon if help is needed.

### Recent History

While the Thelas were not exactly invited to take over the Port’s operations at the end of the 4th Corporate War, no one complained when the facilities got rebuilt and food started flowing in. Now that things are stabilizing, the corporations want to return to business as usual. They haven’t gone so far as to demand that the Thelas hand operation of the Port to city officials, but the corporations have built two alternative cargo transfer facilities: the Heywood Docks and the Ebunike Docks. Neither offers the security and safety of the Port of Night City, but the docks have siphoned off some of our business. The whole mess grew worse last year, when a Decker, Tanaka & Rogers flotilla steamed into the Port and refused to pay its fees. It was a test to see how firm the Thelas' grasp was. If we hadn’t been able to force them to pay, it would have weakened our position on the City Council - and might even have given the Council an excuse to award control of the Port of Night City to someone else. Luckily, things went in our favor this time. Next time, we might not be so lucky. History isn’t exactly kind to nomads.
I should also mention the Russian mob, known locally as the Consortium, and their allies the Vyriy, a Russian nomad organization. They moved in shortly after we reopened the Port. The Thelas and the Consortium battled in the shadows for years over control of the district. The Thelas came out on top, but the cold war didn’t end until recently, when Calypso and the Consortium’s boss, Valya, made peace so they could face the bigger threat: the corporations.

## Reclamation Zone

### City Manager

The current City Manager of the Reclamation Zone is Santos Dorado (Nomad 4/Exec 6 ), the head of the Dorado Clan. They say he was born on the rails, and his heart beats to the rhythm of old steam trains. He just can’t keep up anymore. People in the community are quietly lining up behind their favored candidate in anticipation of an election for a new City Manager. I just hope it remains cordial. The pursuit of power has a funny way of screwing with people’s heads.

### Population

Most of the Reclamation Zone’s population is part of the Dorado Clan, former nomads who left the Snake Nation to rebuild this part of Night City. Despite the name, not everyone in the Dorado Clan is related. It's actually a large group made up of multiple families. Roughly half of the Clan is Muslim, descended from a community that formed in Mexico during the 1980s. The other half is fairly agnostic, though a few travel to Little Europe or Laguna Bend to attend mass at on holidays.
Around ten percent of the people living in the Reclamation Zone joined up with the Dorados after the Clan settled the district. They include Techs and drivers who worked the NCART system before the Dorados took over, locals who lived here when the Dorados moved in, and former nomads who grew disillusioned with the mobile lifestyle … like my folks.

### Security Provider

Security in the Reclamation Zone is provided by Los Perros Guardianes, a mostly volunteer militia. Almost every adult here receives training and serves as an auxiliary member, but a small number of dedicated folk form the day-to-day core. They fulfill their mandate with the help of trained K-9 units. The mission of Los Perros Guardianes is simple: stop trouble before it gets so big that NCPD feels it needs to step in. No one wants Night City’s "finest" to roll through our district.

### Recent History

The Reclamation Zone is one of Night City’s youngest districts, so pretty much everything here is recent history!
Our story begins and ends with the Dorado Clan, formerly Snake Nation nomads. The Clan used to maintain the Zephyr railway in Southern California before the contract was bought out from under them by a Meta clan.
Santos Dorado, the Clan elder, was disheartened by it all. Looking ahead to his final days, he wanted to see his kin grow and prosper, not scrap with other nomads for a piece of a shrinking pie. So, old Santos traveled to Night City, marched right into Night Corp’s headquarters, and brokered a deal. If the city forked over a patch of land for the Dorado Clan, the Dorado Clan would ensure the public transportation system operated like a well-oiled machine.
Hands were shook and contracts signed. By a slim margin, the City Council voted to carve off a slice of South Night City and award it to the Dorados as a new district named the Reclamation Zone. We’ve been building ever since, despite occasional acts of sabotage and semi-regular invasions by the Kill Krashers from the east. We suspect Gavin Haakensen, the City Manager of South Night City, is behind both. He wasn’t happy when the Council voted to take away some of his territory and award it to the Dorados.

## Old Combat Zone

The project’s managers promised jobs, opportunities, and, most importantly, revenue for the corporate partners who bought in. What they received, instead, was the 4th Corporate War.
I’ll spare you the details. Just know, within the first few months of the conflict between Arasaka and Militech, the shiny new San Morro Industrial Zone was decimated. It became, as war reporter C.J. O’Reilly famously quipped, "more of a combat zone than the damn Combat Zone!"
Following the war, money was spent to build The Glen.
Little Europe. The Upper Marina. Reconstruction of the San Morro Industrial Zone sank to the bottom of the priority list, behind even the Hot Zone. Its edges had already sloughed off into the bay, thanks to the liquefaction caused by the nuke. It was a lost cause.
Enter Lasky (Solo 6), a Solo who saw too much horror during the 4CW. He gathered a group of like-minded veterans and refugees and abandoned civilization. His exact words: "The corporations will rebuild, but they won’t learn.
This will all happen again. I’m too faded to care. I’m taking my people into that Old Combat Zone, where no one will bother us. The rest of the world can go to hell."
For the last two decades, the people of the Old Combat Zone have scrounged, gathered, and rebuilt, transforming the district into a warren of shored-up buildings, high-density structures, and isolated fortresses. From the outside, the OCZ looks like a post-apocalyptic movie set, where survivors desperately create refuge from the scraps of a pulverized civilization. Inside, it may seem ramshackle and rough-hewn, but it is a home. One we take great pride in.

### City Manager

Until three years ago, the Old Combat Zone had no City Manager. It was not, technically speaking, even a district of Night City. That changed when Brick Coleman (Solo 7), a child of the OCZ, returned from his turn as a mercenary with enough money to purchase a seat on the City Council. Brick was orphaned by the nuke and raised by Lasky and the people of the OCZ. As a child, he was a sponge, absorbing everything his elders could teach him. When he reached maturity, he traveled out into the world and sold his skills. What he witnessed out there convinced him that the people of the OCZ could not stay isolated. If we did not rise and take control of our own destiny, others would eventually crush us and steal our homes. Brick Coleman named his crusade Edgerunners Inc., after an old group that Lasky told stories around the fire about. Brick has only one goal: to ensure the financial, political, and cultural future of the Old Combat Zone and its residents so that no one will develop our land but us.

### Population

The majority of the OCZ’s population is split along a generational divide. Older residents, known as the Faded, believe in Lasky’s original vision: keep the Old Combat Zone to the Old Combat Zone and leave the rest of the city to its own devices. They mostly stay in the Warren, these days. Younger residents, who have proudly adopted the moniker Edgerunners Inc. (ERI for short), believe in Brick’s vision of an OCZ as an integrated part of Night City run for, and by, the people who live there. They’ve spread out, rebuilding and reclaiming buildings outside the Warren.
There’s one other major faction in the Old Combat Zone, the Iron Sights, who control territory on the north side of the district. The gang uses it as a rearguard base in its war against the Red Chrome Legion.

### Security Provider

Outside the Warren, the security provider for the OCZ is Edgerunners Inc., though obviously not every member of Brick’s faction is deputized to keep the peace and make arrests. A core force of official peacekeepers, organized by Brick, patrols the tunnels and streets of the Old Combat Zone in squads. Most squads consist of one older, experienced Solo and a team of five to ten newer recruits.
Edgerunners Inc. doesn’t imprison people. If someone is caught causing mischief, they’re given a warning.
Those who steal are escorted to the edge of the OCZ and warned not to return. Those who maim, murder, or rape are executed on the spot. Justice here is brutal, but it works. Inside the Warren, the Faded provide their own security. The results of causing trouble are much the same, but punishment is dealt out by the people, not a deputized security force.

### Recent History

Brick’s efforts to build the OCZ into a functioning Night City district dominate conversation here. The construction and successful operation of Substation 1 2 is a shining beacon of his success, proving to the world that the OCZ is more than broken buildings. Or so we like to think.
Tension between the Faded and Edgerunners Inc. has risen in recent months due to a proposal made by Garven Haakensen, the City Manager of South Night City. He has suggested tearing down the Warren and constructing a Megabuilding in its stead. Brick has, thus far, fought the proposal, but many in his faction believe it is a solid next step for building up the district - as long as the residents of the Warren are relocated into the Megabuilding after its construction begins. The Faded like their home the way it is, and Lasky has vowed to shoot any deconstruction crews that roll up. Brawls between the Faded and the ERI are growing more common each day.
Matters are complicated by the Iron Sights. As the war with the Red Chrome Legion winds down, Iron Sights leaders are searching for a new challenge. Members have begun pushing beyond the southern edges of the gang’s territory and further into the OCZ, hoping to expand their influence.

They are a tightly knit clan of isolationists who cultivate fungus and lichen in massive subterranean gardens, both to subsist on and to sell through Mrs. Suzuki’s Bodega in Old Japantown.
The Shroomers are friendly, if a bit spooky. If your journeys take you underground, they are best approached with care and courtesy. Respect them, and they will offer wary hospitality in return. Don’t, and you’ll likely find yourself subject to a fatal fungal infection. The Shroomers are descended from folks who studied Cordyceps while growing psychotropic mushrooms - plan accordingly.
If you wish to know more about the Shroomers, you can often find a few living in the Old Combat Zone. It is tradition for their young to go on walkabout for a year upon reaching adulthood. This allows them to decide whether Shroomer life is truly for them and to find mates to bring back to the family.

#### #16 The Warren
**Security Code 8:** The Faded, Hardened Medium ◊ Utility Code: 7
The Warren began life as the remains of disparate buildings - robotic factories and high-density worker housing.
Following the war, they were occupied, hardened, and expanded by the Faded until they coalesced into one massive superstructure … the first true megabuilding in Night City, if you will, and damn anyone who suggests the old New Harbor Mallplex was the first, instead.Just thinking about the Warren likely gives architects the sweats. It is a massive fortress covering multiple city blocks. Inside, a maze of random stairways, twisting hallways, converted housing, and community spaces offers additional protection. Gangs and corporate strike teams who attempt a raid on the Warren find it almost impossible to navigate … and if they make any progress, they encounter experienced and armed resistance behind almost every door.
Amazingly self-sufficient, the Warren holds within its walls markets, schools, clinics, armories, shops, hydroponic gardens, and even small factories. Many Faded who live inside minimize contact with the outside world.
Members of Edgerunners Inc. are welcomed inside, and some still live there with their families, though increasing tensions have driven some to leave.
Outsiders are allowed to visit and do business here, but usually only on the outer ring of the make shift fortress.
Newcomers seeking to live in the Warren must prove themselves to the locals first. A small tent city of hopefuls is set up just outside the complex.

◊ A. Angel Radio: My little corner of the world. From
this repurposed penthouse suite I broadcast news, information, and the occasional joke to the people of the Old Combat Zone. Mind the floor if you visit. The whole room is slanted due to structural issues.
◊ B. Baskin Books: A small shop selling what Leo
Baskin, the owner, lovingly refers to as the "most used" books in Night City. The locals jest, saying Leo pays the allowances of every kid in the Old Combat Zone, since he gives them 1eb for each book they bring him, no matter its condition. Many residents of the OCZ earned their first "paycheck" by selling Leo books dug out of the district’s wreckage.
◊ C. OCZ Schoolhouse: Originally designed as a
factory cafeteria, the space now serves as an educational facility for the children of the Old Combat Zone. Ms. Slee (Solo 5), the founder, is the school's principal and primary enforcer, and personally teaches every self-defense class the kids take. Reading, writing, arithmetic, and small-arms fire are taught by

community volunteers, as well as by the occasional trusted outsider. The OCZ Schoolhouse is one of the few locations in the Warren actively guarded by a squad from Edgerunners Inc. Everyone agrees, whatever arguments the community might have, the children need the best possible protection.
◊ D. The Pantheon: A non-denominational sacred
space open to followers of all religions, the Pantheon was once an abandoned visitor’s center before the Warren’s expansion subsumed it. Any spiritual or religious leader can book the Pantheon for services.
In a single week, it might host a Methodist sermon, a Buddhist meditation practice, a Wiccan sabbat, a Jewish holy day celebration, and a Reckoner lecture on the Harvest of Souls. When not in use for services, the Pantheon remains open to the public for prayer and meditation. Beyond its availability to all religions, the Pantheon’s largest attraction is its library. Sister Agatha, the Pantheon’s caretaker, collects braindance recordings of religious practices. Devotees speaking in tongues. Zen masters meditating. Nuns in deep prayer. That sort of thing. With her permission, visitors can slot the BDs in the collection, though they must do so on site.
◊ E. Smithery: The Smithery is a shop dedicated to a
rotating selection of unique firearms, including some reproductions of models no longer available on the first-hand market, all forged by gunsmiths who cut their teeth making weapons for the Corporate Wars.
The skilled Techs who own the Smithery take pride in their work, crafting not just weapons, but artistic masterpieces. Because of this, outsiders shopping here pay roughly three times the going rate for a gun. They usually do so willingly because of the beauty of each piece. OCZ residents pay the standard market value.
◊ F. Uncle Anne’s: A small stall promising "real meat
every day" and "Kibble by the bag." I recommend not inquiring as to where either is sourced.
