There was never a question. Of course, when we began work on *Cyberpunk RED*, we knew we would also publish *Night City 2045*. After all, in many ways, Night City is the main character of the Cyberpunk world! Truth be told, it took us longer than we expected, but when you read through this massive tome, we think you'll understand why. It isn't just a list of locations, but a living, breathing experience waiting for you to explore. Welcome to Night City. We're thrilled to share it with you!

## Using This Book

How you utilize *Night City 2045* is up to you. As is tradition, we've made it an "in-world" guide entitled *An Outsider's Guide to Night City* – a book compiled by independent Medias to give readers an unsanitized view of the city. In theory, it is available for purchase in the game and something your Edgerunner can open up and extract information from with a good Library Search Skill Check. Just be warned, not everything in this book is true. What's written on the page might be different from what a Crew encounters during play. As we say in the RTG offices, "every table is different" and your facts override our facts.

Once you get past the introductory bit you are currently reading, the "in-world" portion of *Night City 2045* begins. It starts off with general information. First history, geography, travel, laws, services, utilities, media; then Flashmaps offering a look at Night City's railways, hotspots, housing, markets, and medical facilities. That's followed by information on Night City's gangs, organized crime outfits, and security firms.

Finally, the meat of the book, as we travel through Night City. The guide breaks down Night City into four distinct areas: The Island, the Northside, the Mainland, and the Southside, each of which contains multiple districts. With every district, we explore who is in charge, who lives there, who provides security, and any recent history before escorting you — location by location — through the district. At the end of each district share short biographies of interesting and important personalities living/working and archetypal statblocks for NPCs you might run into. The Outskirts and Playland by the Sea also receive entries, so you can take the fun outside the borders of Night City.

## A Moment in Time

*Cyberpunk RED* doesn't run on a meta plot, but we also can't ignore events that have occurred during published material, such as *Tales of the RED: Street Stories* and *Tales of the RED: Hope Reborn*.

With that in mind, this guide "takes place" during *Hope Reborn*, specifically between the *Hope's Calling!!!* and *Ripping the Ripper* missions. Beware potential spoilers.

If you don't know what that means, don't worry. For the most part, it shouldn't affect how you play. Just know you might need to make adjustments if you want to run published missions. For example, the description of the Bozos in *Night City 2045* assumes the events of *Hilaria 2045* – a screamsheet mission from the *Cyberpunk RED Data Pack* – have occurred and the great clown civil war has begun. Change as needed, and we apologize for any inconvenience this causes.

## Your Night City

As you read through *Night City 2045*, you might notice "empty" space between locations on the maps. That's absolutely on purpose! Night City is an elastic metropolis. We don't give it specific dimensions for a reason. Like Gotham City, Gravity Falls, and Midsomer County, it can grow and expand as the story dictates. The open areas between locations on the map exist for you to fill in. Don't be afraid to create new buildings, roads, neighborhoods, or even entire districts of your own as the need arises!

## Dark City, Dark Times

Night City is a dark place, and this book includes quite a few sensitive and/or horrible topics. Violence, sex, drugs, murder, enslavement, cannibalism, and crushing poverty to name a few. Please be aware as you read.

## The Rules

While *Night City 2045* exists primarily as a lore book, offering an "in-world" glimpse of our favorite Cyberpunk metropolis, there is some rules text, highlighted in red – boxes for longer sections or simply red words inside a lore paragraph for short bursts of information. Character names will be followed by a Role and Rank, where applicable. At the end of each district entry, you will also find stat blocks for gangers, security agents, and other generic residents a Crew might encounter in the area. Then there are the codes …

## Security Codes

When something happens in Night City, what's the chance local security will respond? That's what a Security Code is for. Each code is written as follows:

**Security Code #: \<Faction\>, \<Intensity\>**

**#** represents the likelihood that security for a district or location will respond. To determine if an incident triggers a response, roll 1d10 and compare it to the Security Code #. If the roll is less than the Code #, a response occurs.

**Faction** indicates which group is most likely to respond to an incident at a location, whether it be a gang, NCPD, or a hired security contractor.

**Intensity** gives a strength rating for potential responses. If an intensity listing is Hardened, this means the responders should be hardened according to the rules in the Hardened NPC series, available for free at the RTG website.

Each district has a Security Code. This indicates the local authority's general likelihood of responding to events on the street. Each individual location also has a Security Code, representing either on-site protection or a variation of interest from district authorities – the cops tend to respond with more alacrity to a home belonging to a wealthy patron than to a run-down cube hotel. Note, not all responders have arrest powers. Some will just attempt to drive off or beat the snot out of offenders.

Gamemasters, Security Codes are guidelines, not rules. You decide what might trigger a response – whether it be walking around a high security district while openly carrying massive weapons, shooting a random passerby, or tripping a silent alarm. That's your call – sometimes you want the extra complication to stretch out a beat … and sometimes you just want to avoid the hassle and move onto the new scene. Common sense applies, too. If a place has security guards, they'll probably be present at the front doors.

It is also your call when it comes to response time. Higher Security Codes usually translate into faster responses, but unless the Crew is blowing up cars right in front of a police cordon, it still takes time to scramble and travel. Don't be afraid to change things up, either. A business might have hired 6th Street for protection, but if the Crew causes enough trouble, NCPD is sure to show up. Hell, someone might even call in MAX-TAC to take care of "rampaging cyberpsychos" if the mess is big enough! If you absolutely need a random response time, roll 1d6 for quick response, 1d10 for slow response. The number rolled tells you how many minutes it will take.

Security responses make for good complications. Sirens in the distance and blaring alarms add tension to a situation, but the cops showing up also changes the dynamic of play. Use this system to ramp up tension, set expectations for a location, and spice up play, but don't let it derail your game by turning a simple heist into Butch and Sundance's last stand … unless that's the perfect way to end the session.

### Security Code Intensity

| Intensity | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| **Light** | Mooks and the occasional Lieutenant will respond to the incident. Treat the Crew as having one to two less members for the purposes of calculating combat difficulty (see CP:R page 399). |
| **Medium** | Mooks and Lieutenants respond to the incident. Occasionally, Minibosses also respond. Calculate combat difficulty as normal. |
| **Heavy** | No restrictions on enemy type. Treat the Crew as having at least one additional member for the purposes of calculating combat difficulty. |

**Example:** The Lucky Charms have been hired by Continental Brands to destroy a rooftop guerrilla garden in an apartment building. They haven't been subtle about it and made enough noise the GM decides someone probably called the cops. Checking the location in *Night City 2045*, the GM notices the building has the following Security Code:

**Security Code 5: 6th Street, Hardened Light.**

The GM rolls 1d10 and it comes up as a 4. He decides 6th Street is sending a patrol car to the location and they'll arrive in three minutes (60 Rounds). They ask the Players for Local Expert Checks. One succeeds, so the GM informs them that the average response time for deputized security in this district is three minutes … they may want to hurry up. While the Crew continues, the GM pulls out some NPC stat blocks, just in case. The Crew has six members, so the GM decides four 6th Street Mooks will pull up in a van after the three minute mark and set up shop outside the main exit, ready to shoot at anyone who emerges. From here, play determines how the scene proceeds. What happens next depends on how the Players respond.

## Utility Codes

In addition to a Security Code, each district also has a Utility Code. This represents the likelihood that a location's utilities (power, water, and CitiNet) are functioning at any given moment. To determine if a location has power/water/CitiNet access, roll 1d10 and compare it to the Utility Code #. If the roll is less than the #, the utility in question isn't currently working. In most cases, a location uses the same Utility Code as its district, but occasionally there might be differences. If so, it will be noted in the location's listing.

We recommend using Utility Codes to add flavor. Don't use Utility Codes on missions unless you need an extra complication to increase the challenge rating of the job.

**Example:** The Lucky Charms managed to escape from 6th Street, fleeing north and taking shelter in their headquarters, located in the bowels of Megabuilding H10. The Crew is worried about being hunted down by 6th Street or, worse, NCPD, so they want to log into the CitiNet with their Agents and scan to see if a bulletin has been issued with their information on it. The GM decides this is a good time to build tension by using the Watson Development's Utility Code (3). They roll 1d10 and it comes up a 2. There is currently a CitiNet blackout in their area. Without more information, the Lucky Charms don't know if it is safe for them to leave Megabuilding H10, meet with their Fixer, and get paid. After all, NCPD Precinct #3 is only a few blocks away. The cops could be anywhere!
