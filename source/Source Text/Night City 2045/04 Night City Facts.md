Richard Night promoted it as *The City on the Edge of Tomorrow*. Tourist brochures sell it as *The Crossroads of the West Coast*. When I’m deep in my cups and feeling poetic, I call it *The Blighted City of Possibilities*. My name is Trace Santiago (Media 6); I’m a Media running an independent investigative outfit named Leak News. I’m also a nomad – a proud member of the Aldecaldo Nation. I’ve picked fruit on farms outside Mexico City, welded steel high above San Francisco, and worked overwatch for caravans running up to Vancouver. All cities with more land, more buildings, and more people, but none of them draw me in like Night City. None of them embody this dark, fucked up future we live in like Richard Night’s orphaned only child.
The genesis of this guide lies with my niece. I was spending time with her family as they traveled the nomad road from Arcadia in Nevada to Night City. Her teacher had assigned her an essay comparing and contrasting the two endpoints of the journey. Naturally, she asked her uncle about Night City, and I shared what I knew. The exchange got me thinking, outsiders come to Night City every year, and the information available on the Data Pool and from official sources can be … polished. It doesn’t portray the city the way people on The Street experience it – raw, dirty, disturbing, vibrant, and dangerous. Those living on The Edge of society needed their own guide to help them navigate the realities of Night City.

Upon arriving in Night City, I shared breakfast with my niece and her family, then returned to the base I share with my crew. I made calls and touched base with some of the best indie Medias the city has to offer. Asked them a question: would they write about the Night City they knew?
That’s what you’re reading – or listening to – now. *An Outsider’s Guide to Night City*. Read it with a questioning mind. Hell, read it with a skeptical mind. Independent Medias are an opinionated bunch. We’re gonzo journalists, covering what we feel needs covering with a lens wiped clean of corporate shit. Bias leaks through with that kind of reporting. That’s why each person writing for this guide introduces themselves – me included. To give you context. So you can decide whether you agree with an opinion presented or want to delete the whole file.
Either way, you’ll learn something about Night City.
Trace Santiago

## A History of Night City

Transcribed from a lecture given by Professor Rip R. Jackson

Listen up! As part of our continuing tour of the train wreck that is modern history, the topic of today’s lecture is Night City, from founding to the present. Everyone hear me okay?
Good. Take notes. Everything is fair game for the final.
The story of Night City begins with Richard Night, a man with a dream. Good dream? Bad dream? You decide.
Concerned with the violence and disruption plaguing the United States as it descended into Collapse, Night did what any self-respecting overconfident visionary would do: he planned a new city and engineered it to be an independent, defensible, shining beacon of enlightened capitalism for the world to admire. He took advantage of a collapsing real estate market thanks to the general crap state of the world and a massacre in Morro Bay, California, to buy up land cheap. Then he lured in megacorporations like Arasaka, Petrochem, and Merill, Asukaga & Finch to help clean up and finance the building of his dream: Coronado City. Yeah, that’s right. Man had an ego, for sure, but for whatever reason, he didn’t name the city after himself. We’ll get to the renaming later.

### Night Falls on Coronado City

Thanks to Night’s architectural know-how and a fracking obscene amount of money, Coronado City was a miraculous feat of civil and geological engineering. The surrounding hills were leveled and used as fill – dumped into the bay to create a new landmass. The harbor was dredged to accommodate the large ships needed to carry materials and goods to and from the city. Taking inspiration from the world’s most successful theme parks, Night designed every section of his city as a distinct neighborhood, each with a different architectural style and ambiance. Everything proceeded according to his vision. Plans were drawn in 1991. Construction started in 1993. The city was officially incorporated in 1994.
Everything ran smooth … right up until it didn’t.
Then, just like now, construction companies were controlled by a variety of special interests, including the mob.
Night fought hard to keep mob-backed companies out of his rapidly rising city, but his luck ran out on September 20, 1998, when he was shot and killed in his penthouse suite. Night’s murderer was never caught. Everyone cried.
The city draped itself in mourning. Reps from the founding companies held vigil and changed the burg’s name from Coronado City to Night City in his honor. It was all incredibly moving. So much so that the mob waited a whole week out of respect before moving in.

### The Mob Wars

For the next decade, criminal organizations effectively ruled Night City. In the beginning, they were smart enough to leave the megacorps alone, and in return, the megacorps didn’t interfere with their activities. Safe in their glass-and-steel towers, executives made money hand over fist. Down on the street, Night City devolved into a war zone, with new gangs rising to carve out turf and cybernetic terrorism running rampant. What happened next is what always happens. People got greedy. The mob started messing with megacorp operations – tried to grab a bigger piece of the pie.
Hit in their wallet, the megacorps chose to retaliate with the sort of force usually used to conquer small countries.
By 2011, well-paid mercenary troops had destroyed the criminal underground’s power base in Night City. The megacorps installed a puppet mayor, and the city entered a golden age where crime vanished and the Corporate Center gleamed in accordance with Richard Night’s vision. Or so the official history you’ll read on Night City’s Garden Patch says. Don’t believe it.

### The Early 21st Century

With a firm grip on the city, the megacorps ensured every law passed benefited them, even when it was dressed up as "good for the community." Money for municipal projects flowed to districts already flush with cash while poorer neighborhoods deteriorated. A combat zone formed in Night City – a place where cops and corps dared not tread. Every day, its borders expanded by inches. Gangs in the zone acted with impunity. The megacorps didn’t care so long as their profit margins kept rising. Hell, sometimes the gangs were useful in stirring up public support for corporate policies or cracking down on rivals. More than a few during this time were corporate-backed. Assholes might as well have been clocking in and out for the day at megacorp HQ.
Besides the rich, another class of people benefited during the first few decades of the 21st century: edgerunners. While the megacorps publicly cooperated for "the good of Night City," they plotted and schemed against each other behind the PR wall. Hiring edgerunners to carry out missions of sabotage, kidnapping, theft, and murder accomplished executive goals but gave the C-suite crowd plausible deniability should something go wrong. The classic gig cycle – client to Fixer to edgerunner – was established at this time, and it continues to this day.

### The 4th Corporate War

2022. Arasaka and Militech start clawing each other’s eyes out in the 4th Corporate War. Night City was Arasaka’s stronghold in North America, so of course, the corporate titans took their fight to our streets. Thousands fled. Other megacorps pulled up stakes and moved to safer cities. Street-to-street fighting was a daily occurrence. Arasaka struck Militech assets. Militech struck Arasaka assets. Gangs, juiced up by the violence, struck everyone’s assets. Things got so bad, the United States government finally grew a fracking spine after decades of corporate proxy control. The US military moved in and surrounded Night City, ready to beat the crap out of Arasaka on its home turf – but it never invaded.

You know why. August 20, 2023. A nuclear explosion obliterated Arasaka's Night City headquarters and devastated the Corporate Center. It also sent ripples through the Island's geology, liquefacting the fill it was built on. Night City experienced months of war, followed by a nuclear blast, followed by what was effectively an earthquake.
Just who set off the bomb remains a mystery to this day. Ask ten people, and you'll get eleven different answers. The United States maintains that Arasaka caused the explosion as part of a denial strategy. Arasaka blames Militech/USA-backed terrorists led by Johnny Silverhand, a damn Rockerboy of all things. I've heard rumors that other players were involved. Morgan Blackhand. Aldecaldo Lobos. Angels from the Crystal Palace. Truth is, we'll probably never know the truth. The evidence of what happened died the same day the nuke went off. In the end, it probably doesn't matter. Who did it wouldn't change the end result: Night City almost died.

### The Time of the Red

Following the war, Night City struggled to rebuild, and not just from the nuke. Bartmoss’s DataKrash shut down the Old Net. Self-replicating AI seeker mines clogged the ocean’s shipping lanes. Reliable knowledge was hard to come by, and the supply chains were fracked. For a while, it seemed as if Night City wouldn’t survive. Parts of it, maybe, as local leaders and wannabe warlords began building their own personal fiefdoms, but the city as a whole? It wasn’t looking good.
Enter Miriam Night and Night Corp. Miriam claimed to be Richard Night’s widow, which shocked everyone because no one even knew the choombatta was married! Still, she acted with the same confidence as old Richard, and more importantly, she had an obscene amount of money and was willing to spend it on any local fiefdom willing to sign on to her plan to rebuild her late husband’s city.
In 2028, various power brokers and leaders came together and reforged Night City with a new city charter.
The charter divided the city into new districts, putting City Managers in charge of each and bringing them together as a Council to make city-wide laws and decisions – this kept power in the hands of the elite and prevented a centralized authority, like a mayor bought and paid for by a single corporation, from overshadowing the Council.
With a charter in place, Night Corp led the rebuilding process. First, it lured the nomads in. They came en masse, taking on contracts and hauling their own supplies. Having obtained expertise by helping to rebuild Los Angeles, Chicago, and Mexico City in the past, the nomads, led by the Aldecaldos, proved vital. Next were the megacorporations. Night City was founded on the concept of business, and it needed the engines of capitalism to remain present and engaged in order to survive. According to some well-publicized news reports of the time, Miriam Night personally braved the war-ravaged wilds of the world to meet with the CEOs of Biotechnica, Petrochem, Network 54, and Trauma Team. Those were the first corporations to return to Night City. Others followed as reconstruction proceeded, often at a snail's pace.

### Disruptions

Getting the materials needed wasn’t easy. It took time for the nomads to establish secure supply routes – first regionally, then across the continent, and finally, a scant few across the ocean. The City Council was also a factor.
Being a political body without an effective leader, constant infighting, backstabbing, and power plays slowed down its ability to make effective decisions. Sprinkle in regular violence thanks to corporate shenanigans, gang warfare, and the fact that there are more guns than people in Night City, and you’ve got a recipe for the occasional disruption of rebuilding efforts. Then there’s the disasters, natural or otherwise.
In 2029, a doomsday cult called the Second Comming set off dozens of car bombs across Night City, destroying key construction sites. They believed the nuke of 2023 was a sacred event, and rebuilding the city profaned it.
In response, Max Hammerman stepped up and reformed MAX-TAC as a private organization. Net54 ran nothing but footage of "Hammerman and his Lawmen" for weeks as they hunted down and executed every member of the cult, then billed the City Council for it.
In 2033, a 7.3 magnitude earthquake hit the region.
This being California, we’re used to earthquakes, but the Island’s foundation wasn’t fully repaired from the damage done by the nuke. Liquefaction rolled through like a tidal wave, toppling buildings and ripping apart streets.
Reconstruction was set back months, if not years.
Disease isn’t uncommon in Night City, but early in 2036, the Varicella-H (V-H) virus ravaged the population harder than any outbreak since the Wasting Plague.
Everything shut down. Rumors that the outbreak started in the Aldecaldo Camp didn't help. Editorials called the disease "Nomad Pox" and riled up mobs into attacking nomads. It got so bad, the Aldecaldos considered pulling up stakes and moving.
V-H resisted every attempt to prevent or treat it for months. Towards the end of the year, Biotechnica developed a working vaccine. Night Corp, Trauma Team, and Biotechnica joined forces to manufacture and distribute it, marking the first city-wide vaccination campaign since the 4th Corporate War. By mid-2037, hospitalizations and deaths from V-H dropped to "manageable levels". Tempers cooled. Everyone got back to work.
The last major disruption was the Badlands Incident of 2041. Two nomad convoys, one Jodes and one Aldecaldos, met about a day east of Night City. Each was escorting CHOOH2 tankers. Things got heated. Violence ensued. Three tanker trucks exploded, and fifteen people died. For over a month, the leaders of the Jodes and Aldecaldos in Night City struggled to maintain the peace. Construction and transportation crawled as nomads picked sides and prepped for war. It wasn't until Ziggy "Front" Page published an investigative piece that things cooled down. According to eye witness reports and forensic evidence, neither side initiated the Incident. Turns out, the tankers were being run by drivers working for SovOil and Petrochem. They got into a brawl. Guns were pulled, then the whole thing went to hell. The Jodes and Aldecaldos made peace after the big reveal, but the fractures to nomad unity caused by the incident remain to this day.

## General Information

Our fair city was originally a planned urban community, founded in 1994 by the late entrepreneur Richard Alix Night. Facing the Pacific Ocean, Night City was created through a combination of dredging and land reclamation to be a modern city of the twenty-first century – a shining example of technology and human ingenuity triumphant over the troubles of the past.
Modern Night City is anything but what Night intended.
Transformed by decades of street violence, corporate exploitation, war, disasters, and plagues, today’s Night City is a place of contrast, where you can travel from a shining metropolis of stunning opulence to a decaying ruin of crushing poverty just by crossing a street. The population is estimated at around three million, but no one knows for sure. There hasn’t been a true census for decades, and registered citizens are only a portion of the population. At least a third of the people residing in Night City live month to month inside two meters of rented space … when they can afford to pay rent at all. Over half the city eats Kibble at least once per day just to survive.

### Location

Night City is located on the coast at the south end of the Free State of Northern California (NorCal), where it operates as an independent city – that is, it is not part of a larger county. It is a trading and cultural crossroads and serves as a gateway to the Pacifica Confederation – a Night City Timeline
### Free Port

While Night City is part of NorCal (and thus the Pacifica Confederation), it operates with a surprising degree of independence and serves as a "free port," with looser restrictions on corporations. Elsewhere in the Confederation, corporations operate under laws imposed after the disaster of the 4th Corporate War, including so-called "face laws" that make a CEO or regional executive legally responsible for corporate wrongdoing. There’s none of that in Night City – a compromise designed to encourage businesses to invest in the economy and reconstruction of Pacifica Confederation member states. Additionally, Night City does not collect income tax. Instead, it raises funds through licensing, property taxes, corporate contributions, and other fees.

### Weather

The 2020 edition of Fax on File’s Night City guidebook described its climate as "temperate." Decades of climate change, compounded by a flood of gasses and particles into the atmosphere during the 4th Corporate War, makes "unstable" a better adjective when talking about the city’s weather.
The average temperature ranges from the low 40s (4° to 6° Celsius) in the winter months to low 90s (32° to 35° Celsius) in the summer, but extremes dropping into the low 30s (around 0° Celsius) or climbing into the high 110s (around 47° Celsius) aren’t uncommon. Misty fog, often tinged yellow as it picks up various pollutants, blankets the city in the early mornings and late evenings. Precipitation is relatively rare, averaging 19 inches per year. There are approximately two to three thunderstorms per year. The last recorded snowfall was in 2023, shortly after the destruction of the Arasaka Towers. A combination of factors, including coastal positioning, a decades-long drought, the 2023 nuclear explosion, rampant pollution, and a battered worldwide climate, means Night City is home to several unusual weather conditions. In addition to the acid rain and inversion smogs found in many cities, Night City residents and visitors can also expect to encounter the occasional dust storm, blood rain, and radioactive winds. Anyone expecting to spend time out of doors here should consider purchasing personal protective equipment.

### Citizenship

In rebuilding Night City, the government faced a frustrating problem: the near-complete destruction of records due to the DataKrash. Some paper backups existed, but their storage place proved less than ideal when it flooded with sea water due to liquefaction in the aftermath of the bomb.
Records of citizenship, property ownership, marriages, birth, vehicle ownership, licenses … all lost.
Faced with a blank slate, the government passed laws designed to simplify the process of registering for citizenship. To obtain a citizenship and a State Identification Number (SIN) in Night City, go to City Hall and show either a pre-4CW SIN card or a notarized letter of recommendation from a NorCal citizen with a valid SIN card, pay a 100eb (Premium) fee, and register your data – biometric and otherwise. A clerk will then issue you a SIN card.
SIN cards are digital storage devices shaped like a credit card or license. The data on a SIN card includes your actual SIN, various biographical information (name, address, and so forth), criminal records, any licenses granted by the government, and a recording of specific biometric data (retinal pattern, fingerprints, and DNA sample). There are citizenship registration offices attached to the maternity wards of various Night City medical centers for new birth registration. Babies born in such a facility are automatically registered, and the cost is passed on to the parent via an additional processing fee. Anyone arraigned in court for criminal activities in Night City is issued a SIN if they don’t have one. The cost is added to their "justice" bill.
You need a SIN if you want to:
- Get a legal job.
- Interact with the government in any way, including getting a license or registering ownership of real estate, firearms, or a vehicle.
- Vote in district elections (if your district has them).
- File suit in Court (though you do not need a SIN to have a suit filed against you).
- Sign a legally recognized contract.

#### Corporate Identification Numbers

Most large corporations in Night City issue Corporate Identification Numbers (CINs). These act almost identically to SINs but also keep track of employment records (performance reviews, disciplinary actions, and so on) and professional certifications. If a corporation sits on the Night City Council, all employee CIN cards function as SIN cards in Northern California.

#### ID Scanners

ID Scanners are field-portable devices used to scan SIN/ CIN cards for identity verification. They come equipped with a biometric scanner capable of reading retinal patterns, fingerprints, or small blood samples and matching the data against the information on a slotted card. Citizens can purchase an ID Scanner from City Hall for 500eb (Expensive) when they are in stock. The scanner is useless without access to the NorCal Identity Database. A subscription costs 100eb (Premium) per year.

#### Multiple Citizenships

There is no requirement to give up citizenship in another country/state when registering for citizenship in Northern California. It isn’t uncommon for Night City residents to have a NorCal SIN and a CIN, or multiple SINs – one for each country/state they’re registered in. As long as the government makes money off your fees, it doesn’t care.

#### Nomads and Citizenship

Nomads keep meticulous genealogical records but, as a matter of pride, rarely seek official citizenship in a specific locale. Because the nomad nations are heavily involved in Night City in the Time of the Red, a treaty has been struck to avoid friction. Nomads who frequently travel into Night City can stop by AllNations Construction in the Aldecaldo Camp to obtain a Temporary ID Card. These cards only have two pieces of information encoded on them: a name and a fingerprint for use with ID Scanners. The cards are temporary and the data wipes from both the card and the NorCal Identity Database one month after activation – at least, it is supposed to according to the treaty.

### Getting to Night City

Though it is dangerous, travel to and from Night City happens every day.

#### Driving

Night City is on the New Pacific Highway – aka Highway 101 – running north-south, and Interstate 9 running east-west. Both are open to civilian traffic, but caution is advised. NorCal Marshals patrol both highways up to the state’s borders, but Raffen Shiv and bandit attacks still occur regularly. Travelers hoping for safety should join a nomad caravan, which leave from the Aldecaldos and Jodes Camps several times a week. The fee runs from 100eb (Premium) if you bring your own vehicle and supplies, to 500eb (Expensive) if they need to provide food, water, and a ride. Discounts are offered to skilled individuals willing to work.

#### Air

Just outside Night City is the Jackson Plains Regional Airport. Flights depart/arrive daily for/from Los Angeles, Portland, Seattle, and Vancouver with tickets averaging 500eb (Expensive) per seat for economy and 1,000eb (Very Expensive) per seat for first class. Connections can be made at those airports to continue to other locations.
Private charters for longer trips, including orbital flights, are available but usually start at 5,000eb (Luxury) per passenger.
Two Folk Nation Sky Queen aerozeps, the Milwaukee and the Omaha, stop in Night City once per month. These floating leviathans act as airborne cruise ships, offering luxury and security with gorgeously appointed staterooms, grand dining rooms, and recreational facilities. The Milwaukee flies from Night City to Washington D.C., with stops in Las Vegas, Phoenix, Albuquerque, Denver, and Jefferson City. The Omaha flies from Vancouver to Mexico City, with stops in Seattle, Portland, Night City, Los Angeles, Chihuahua, and Mazatlán. Tickets cost 100eb (Premium) per stop for steerage and 500eb (Expensive) per stop for first class. These are languid vacation flights, averaging 30 days round-trip due to long stays at each stop to allow tourists to explore the sights.

#### Rail

The Snake Nation includes at least one passenger car on most cargo trains they run through Night City. The trains follow a path similar to the Milwaukee and Omaha aerozeps, with additional, minor stops in smaller cities and reclaimer towns along the way. Tickets average 20eb (Everyday) per major stop for a bench seat or 50eb (Costly) per stop for a sleeper car. It takes roughly four days for those trains to run from end to end.

#### Water

The Thelas operate a long-distance ferry service that runs along the west coast of North and Central America from Anchorage to Acapulco, with stops in Prince Rupert, Vancouver, Port of Seattle, Coos Bay, San Francisco, Night City, Los Angeles, and Mazatlán. Additional stops require negotiation with the ferry’s captain. Tickets average 20eb (Everyday) per major stop for a cot in the cargo hold or 50eb (Costly) per stop for a cabin. The trip takes approximately 10 days, end to end, but the duration can change dramatically based on weather conditions.
Trips across the Pacific require chartering a custom berth with a specific ship’s captain, usually Thelas, Darangen, or Vyriy sea nomads. A berth averages 500eb (Expensive) per person, but discounts are offered for passengers willing to work.

### Getting Around Night City

Navigating Night City can be a pain in the ass. This information won’t make it easy – but it will make it possible.

#### If You’re Driving/Piloting

… Night City observes all NorCal air, sea, and land traffic regulations, which are available via the Data Pool.
Residents and visitors to Night City are responsible for knowing and understanding those regulations. NorCal Marshals, the NCPD, and deputized law enforcement units are empowered to use maximum force if necessary to uphold the law. Average Travel Time per District: 15 minutes (Security Code 6 or higher) ♦ 20 minutes (Security Code 4 to 5) ♦ 30 minutes (Security Code 3 or lower). Halve the time (round up) for AV or gyro travel. Triple it for walking.

#### Licenses

To legally operate a vehicle, you must possess a valid license for the class of vehicle you intend to drive or pilot.
All citizens over the age of sixteen can acquire a NorCal driver’s or small watercraft pilot’s license from the Night City DMV by presenting their SIN card and paying a 100eb (Premium) fee – no test involved.
Licenses from elsewhere in the Pacifica Confederation are also valid. Licenses from other jurisdictions are not.
Citizens from outside the Pacifica Confederation can obtain a temporary, one-month license by presenting their passport and paying a 50eb (Costly) fee at their consulate, the DMV, or at some hotel concierge desks.
Flying or piloting an aircraft or large watercraft in Night City is another matter. To obtain a pilot’s license, a citizen must present their SIN card at the DMV, pass a practical examination ( DV17 Pilot Air or Sea Vehicle ), and pay a 500eb (Expensive) fee. Pilots’ licenses from elsewhere in the Pacifica Confederation are considered valid in Night City.

#### Plates

All vehicles in the Free State of Northern California must be registered with the DMV within thirty days of purchase.
Registering a land vehicle or small watercraft costs 100eb (Premium). Registering an air vehicle or large watercraft costs 500eb (Expensive). Upon registering, an applicant will receive a plate that must be bolted to an appropriate spot, depending on the vehicle. Plates from elsewhere in the Pacifica Confederation are considered to be equivalent to NorCal plates for this purpose.
Owners of vehicles from outside the borders of the Confederation can pay a 50eb (Costly) fee (land vehicles and small watercraft) or 100eb (Premium) fee (air vehicles and large watercraft) for a temporary, one-month tag. Only citizens with SIN cards or visitors with legal passports can register a vehicle.

#### Traffic

According to records gleaned from the DMV, between fifteen and twenty-two percent of Night City citizens own a motorized vehicle – a number projected to rise as cheaper options like the Zonda Metrocar become available. Despite this, traffic can be fierce in well-developed areas ( Security Code 6 or higher ) and on major thoroughfares (marked in yellow on the map) due to the enormous number of vehicles owned by nomads, corporations, and the city itself. In combat zones and the outskirts, congestion eases, but proceed with caution. Poor road maintenance makes driving in these areas tricky.
There’s always a chance of encountering a checkpoint at district borders. Official ones are run by NorCal Marshals, the NCPD, and deputized law enforcement agents to "search for fugitives and contraband." So long as you and your vehicle are legal, you can usually pass through in a few minutes, though "donating" a few eb to the checkpoint officers can speed things up. Unofficial checkpoints are run by gangs – mostly inside combat zones – where the going rate for passage is 10 to 20eb per wheel.

#### Parking

Parking in Night City is a nightmare. Curbside parking is available in secure districts ( Security Code 6 or higher ) for an average of 5eb (Dirt Cheap) per hour – more in high traffic areas. It's often free in less secure districts, but you take your chances when you park on the street.
Parking in a lot or a covered garage usually runs 10eb (Cheap) per hour (50eb [Costly] per day), though secure facilities may charge more. Some apartment buildings and housing complexes provide parking spots to residents.
Check with your landlord.
Pad storage for AVs, helicopters, and gyros averages 20eb (Everyday) per hour (100eb [Premium] per day).
NCPD and deputized organizations can and will tow illegally parked vehicles to the impound lot located behind the Hall of Justice. In addition to any fines for the parking violation, springing your vehicle from lockup costs 500eb (Premium). If you don’t pay within forty-eight hours, the NCPD will legally seize the vehicle and either strip it for parts or sell it.

#### Nomads Traveling in Night City

Thanks to a treaty between the Aldecaldo Nation and the City Council, nomads legally entering Night City have special procedures and protections regarding their licenses and vehicles. Nomads visiting Night City should stop by AllNations Construction in the Aldecaldo Camp. There, they can obtain a temporary ID card and diplomatic plates to bolt onto their vehicle and mark them as legal. The cards and plates must be surrendered to AllNations Construction before leaving Night City.
Just a warning. Nomads take this seriously. If they see a vehicle with stolen diplomatic plates, they’ll figure the choomba in question must truly wish to join the family, so they’ll help by grabbing the thief and dropping them off about 50 miles (80.5 km) east of the city's border with no supplies. Just to give them a proper taste of the wandering life.

#### NCART

NCART provides subway and bus service (formerly provided by NCTC) throughout Night City. The system is reliable, but chaotic because sporadic tunnel flooding and maintenance cause daily route changes. I recommend downloading the NCART app to your Agent and checking it regularly to ensure you know your transfer points in advance. One day, it might be a smooth trip on the train between Watson and The Glen. The next, you might need to transfer from the train to a bus back to the train because the Del Coronado Bay tunnel sprang a leak.
NCPD units patrol most stations. Be aware, some (but not all) buses and train cars are rigged to seal shut and flood with sleep gas if a gunshot is detected or the driver hits the panic button. Riding public transport costs 1eb per stop, but most residents buy a monthly pass for 20eb (Everyday).
Characters with a Lifestyle of Kibble or higher receive a monthly NCART pass for free.

### Government

As dictated by its current charter (ratified in 2028), Night City is governed by a City Council composed of different factions, each with a voting seat at the table. The Council is responsible for passing ordinances and laws concerning Night City as a whole. It is also responsible for dispersing funds for large-scale projects, financing and governing emergency services, managing basic utilities, and maintaining central administrative functions, including the Department of Motor Vehicles, Business Licensing, and the Office of Records.
The City Council officially meets once per week at City Hall in closed-door sessions. City Managers take turns serving as Council President, switching out each month.
The President’s only power is in running meetings, but even that is limited. For example, they can’t strike items off the agenda or refuse to recognize another Council member’s right to speak. There are two types of seats available on the City Council: Manager Seats and Advisory Seats.

#### Manager Seats

The Night City Charter recognizes a district as part of the city so long as its City Manager pays a yearly fee to the city. Where this fee comes from doesn’t matter.
The fee can come out of the Manager’s pocket, from a sponsor organization, or gathered via a fee scheme. The City Council doesn’t care where the ebs come from, so long as laws aren’t openly broken during the collection process. If multiple potential City Managers are vying for a seat, the Council awards it to whoever bids the highest.
City Managers are responsible for governing their district. This includes the right to zone, collect fees in accordance with city law, and award contracts for various services. They also have the power to deputize a licensed security provider to operate as a freelance law enforcement agency within the district. City Managers usually attend City Council meetings in person, but can send an authorized proxy if they so desire.

#### Advisory Seats

Any person or organization that can pay the astronomically high yearly fee can buy an Advisory Seat. Despite the name, Council Members with Advisory Seats don’t just advise – they have full voting rights. Technically, a person or group holding an Advisory Seat cannot also directly finance another Seat (Advisory or Manager), but they can belong to another organization that does. For example, multiple corporations with Advisory Seats are also part of the Arroyo Concern, the group that funds the Manager Seat for the Heywood Industrial Zone.
Technically, an Advisory Seat is held by the highest-ranking local officer in an organization, but a proxy is usually sent to meetings in their place. The Council Representative Proxy position is a coveted job in many corporate offices.

### The Law

The state and city legal codes are a freaking mess so complex the government uses an Artificial Intelligence to sort out cases. I’ll try to simplify things as much as possible and stick to the matters readers of this guide would find most important. Let’s start with the cops.

#### NCPD and Private Cops

The NCPD is a municipally run organization responsible for "enforcing the law" and has jurisdiction almost everywhere in Night City. It is funded by and answers directly to the City Council, of which it is also a member via the Patrolman’s Benevolent Association. Despite that membership, NCPD is underfunded and understaffed, so City Managers are authorized under the Charter to deputize a private firm to act as an authorized law enforcement agency in their district.
The contract requires these firms to cooperate with NCPD and other deputized security providers on investigations. The actual amount of cooperation varies, but don’t expect to be free and clear just because you cross a district line. Private cops are still cops. Unless they have a compelling reason not to, chances are they’ll aid their fellow Lawmen in making the arrest.
By law, deputized law enforcement agents can arrest and hold people for up to five days before transferring the perp and all collected evidence to the NCPD for processing. In practice, only a fraction of those arrested by private cops end up in the justice system.
Some agencies encourage "criminals" to pay a "fine" for release before a transfer can occur, and there is little oversight into "escapes," "accidental deaths," and "suicides while in custody." The exception to all the rules is the NorCal Military Base.
Though technically a district of Night City, neither NCPD nor city laws matter past the front gate. The COG Military Police have exclusive jurisdiction over the base and the Uniform Military Code of Justice is the law of the land.

#### Criminal Investigations

The majority of deputized private firms aren’t properly equipped or trained for criminal investigation, and often turn over major cases to city cops. NCPD detectives and forensic investigators are properly equipped and trained, but also overworked. Priority is given to cases involving wealthy victims or intense public interest and publicity.
Private donations to the department are a good way to ensure a case’s file moves to "the top of the stack."

#### Weapons, Drugs & XBDs

Considering the target audience of this guide, the topics of weapons, drugs, and XBDs deserve special note.
Weapons: The weapon laws of NorCal (and thus Night City) are based on the US Federal Weapons Statute of 1999. Only citizens are legally permitted to carry a weapon. Leeway is given to visitors with proper credentials, provided they check in with NCPD. Non-automatic handguns, rifles, and shotguns do not require a license for ownership or open/concealed carry. They also do not need to be registered. Automatic weapons, sniper rifles, and heavy weapons require a license (500eb [Expensive] per year) and registration (100eb [Premium] per weapon).
Grenades and other explosives are illegal without a demolitions license (1,000eb [Very Expensive] per year).
There are no laws governing the ownership or carrying of melee weapons. Ownership and possession laws only cover public areas. Businesses can refuse service or entry to any customer for any reason. Vendors can sell any weapon to legal customers so long as proper fees are paid and licenses are kept up to date.
Drugs: The possession and use of most drugs are perfectly legal in Night City. The only exceptions are "combat-rated" drugs like Berserker, Black Lace, and Prime Time (essentially, any drug that causes Humanity Loss or is used in chemical warfare). Creating or selling legal drugs without a city-issued license is illegal.

XBDs: Creating, possessing, or selling braindance chips with the limiters tuned down beyond government-mandated limits – or removed entirely – is illegal.
Creating or selling legal BDs without a city-issued license is illegal.
The "selling without a license is illegal" concept applies to other "sin services" as well, including gambling and sex work. The government doesn’t mind you doing it, so long as it gets its cut – something freelance operators usually can’t afford. The legal age for drugs and alcohol is 19, by the way.

#### The Process of Justice

Here’s what you’re in for if you fall into the gears of Night City justice. It isn’t at all like what you see in the law dramas on Net54.
Step 1: You’re arrested. No one reads you your rights.
You don't have any. They just cuff you and toss you into a cell in the bowels of the Hall of Justice. Interrogations – often "enhanced" – might occur before your arraignment.
The cops love confessions. With crimes you only pay a fee for, you’re not incarcerated after arrest – simply given a court date. If that’s the case, skip to step 3.
Step 2: Three to five days later, you’re hauled upstairs for arraignment. This is where you’re formally charged, and bail is set. Bail – when set at all – is always high and must be paid at the time of arraignment, which means most people can’t afford it.
Step 3: Roughly a month after arraignment, you’re hauled upstairs again for trial. If someone hasn’t already paid for an attorney to represent you, you go it alone – there are no public defenders in Night City and lawyers rarely volunteer their services for free. There is no insanity plea, and everyone is guilty until proven innocent. Over the next one hour to three days, the prosecution and defense submit information to The Judge (Netrunner 10), an Artificial Intelligence that oversees all civil and criminal cases in Night City. Once the data collection phase is over, The Judge makes a ruling – usually guilty – and imposes a sentence. There is no appeal process.
Step 4: Punishment. Depending on the crime, The Judge can impose one or more of the following penalties:
- A fine, based on the severity of the crime. You can reduce your fine by submitting to a public electro-whipping in the amphitheater outside the Hall of Justice. Each lash ( 3d6 Melee Damage ) lowers the fine by 25eb. If you’re sporting a linear frame and look like you can take it, they might pull out the heavy whip (6d6 Melee Damage ). If you’re thinking of stealing a heavy electro-whip for yourself, don’t bother. They only work if they’re plugged into the wall.
- Detention in the Hall of Justice for a specified period of time. If you’re locked up locally, you probably possess valuable skills. Expect to have a bomb implanted in your brain so you can be "hired out" as slave labor.
- Detention in a state prison for a specified period of time. Usually New Alcatraz or one of the floating prison camps. You’ll probably be plugged into a braindance for up to twelve hours a day and forced to live out the experience of a victim of a crime similar to the one you were convicted of. The rest of the time is for eating, sleeping, and forced labor.
- Execution. To be carried out within forty-eight hours by the City Executioner in the amphitheater outside the Hall of Justice. Tickets will be sold, so try to put on a good show.
- Exile. Occasionally, The Judge sentences someone to exile from Night City. This is usually done when the criminal is both dangerous and of high social or financial standing. Exiles are implanted with a cortex bomb set to trigger if they pass key checkpoints inside the city.
Don’t think successfully hiding from the cops gets you off the hook, either. If it feels it has enough evidence, the city attorney's office can petition The Judge to pass judgment even if you haven’t been arrested. Trial in absentia always results in a death sentence, no matter the crime, to be carried out by the City Executioner – an expert in hunting down and killing people.

### Public Services

Beyond the NCPD and deputized security, several "public" services are available in Night City.

#### 911

Night City’s 911 emergency system is almost entirely automated, thanks to donations from Ziggurat. Calls are guaranteed to connect to a specially-programmed SelfAdaptive Artificial Intelligence (SAAI) that will solicit your SIN, current location, and emergency. It will then hang up and send the information along to the "appropriate authorities," whether that be the fire department, NCPD, or a local security provider. Medical emergency calls are always routed to REO Meatwagon since Trauma Team clients summon care directly. Ziggurat brags that the system has a ninety-seven percent accuracy rating, but an independent investigation by Miller Marcus for his Garden Patch suggests it is closer to seventy percent.

#### Banking

If you’re legal and posh, you’re probably banking with either 1st Night City or EuroBank. Both offer a suite of services, including various account types, loans, stock brokerage, and investment platforms. EuroBank also controls the flow of money in Night City. It is licensed by the NorCal government to monitor local the money stream and to print new bills (both physically and digitally) as needed. The process is run by John Good (Netrunner 10), an AI.
If you’re legal but not posh, you’re more likely to do business with smaller banks, like Heywood Community in Santo Domingo or Mestnyy in the Port of Night City.
They’re usually more willing to loan money to small

businesses, so long as the amount needed is under 50,000eb. If you aren’t legal, you’re stuck with payday loan services like Imperial Bank in Old Japantown or your local loan shark. Good luck.

#### Medical Care

The concept of "average lifespan" in Night City is a joke.
The wealthy can afford to live over a hundred years. The poor die young from violence or any number of treatable injuries or illnesses.
The richest citizens of Night City are cared for in Trauma Team Tower’s client-only hospital, Kosmos Hospital, or the private suites of City Medical. There, they receive the finest care from extensively trained doctors utilizing state-of-theart technology.
Anyone else with money to spend visits smaller facilities, such as Crisis Medical or Hiroto Miyamoto Memorial.
Service there still costs, but the quality depends on which doctor is holding the scalpel and which Tech patched up the equipment.
The rest of the population either has to suffer or hope for the best at their local ripperdoc. They range in quality from top-notch, like Savage Docs, to back-alley butchers like Sara Snow of the Samaritan. Good luck.

#### Education

The City Council abolished the Night City School District in 203 1, placing responsibility for funding and managing primary and secondary education facilities on City Managers. As a result, only the NorCal Military Base, Old Combat Zone, Reclamation Zone, and Santo Domingo operate anything close to traditional public schools.
In other districts, residents can send their children to private academies run by various not-for-profit and for-profit organizations, or purchase an interactive braindance program like Segotari’s ScholarTM Home Learning for at-home education. Most districts offer a subsidy to help citizens afford the interactive braindance option, though the refund paperwork is often onerous. Buying the program at a local Night Market is usually easier.Night City University likes to boast that it is the city’s only institution of higher learning, but that isn’t completely true.
Watson Community College, located in Megabuilding H10, opened last year and offers job-focused educational opportunities on the cheap.

### Utilities and Commerce

Like any modern city, Night City’s utilities are the key to survival. How well those utilities work on any given day depends on which district you’re in. Utility workers making repairs are a common sight around the city – they’re always armed and often guarded by either NCPD or private contractors.
Electricity, water, sewer, and garbage collection fees are covered when you make your Housing payment each month (or permanently, if you buy housing outright). CitiNet access is included as part of your monthly Lifestyle payment.

#### Electricity

Electricity flows into Night City from various sources. The majority of the city’s juice is provided by the PacCon Electric Company via the Jackson Plains Solar Farm and hundreds of wind turbines scattered across the nearby badlands. The combined output of those sources – plus some extra current flowing in from PacCon Electric plants north of Night City – isn’t enough, and blackouts are common. Both Petrochem and SovOil have submitted plans to build a trash-burning power plant. PacCon Electric has offered to build a nuclear power plant inside the city. All three proposals are the subject of endless debate during City Council sessions.

#### Water and Sewer

Night City’s potable water is provided by the Coronado Dam and multiple desalination plants. Individual companies, including Petrochem and Hydrosubsidium, provide the water, while pipe and sewer infrastructure is maintained by the Night City Department of Water. The Thelas operate the reclamation plant in the Port of Night City, treating sewage before returning purified water to the city’s supply. The city’s plumbing infrastructure is particularly vulnerable to the hazards of modern life and is constantly under repair.

#### Garbage Collection

Garbage collection in Night City is handled via private contract. The two most prominent players in the trash removal business are Coastal Carting (a subsidiary of Petrochem) and SMKP Hauling (a subsidiary of SovOil).
How often garbage collectors swing by depends on the company’s contract with a district’s City Manager.
The general rule of thumb is that the posher the district, the more often the trash is removed. Once a day in the Exec Zone; once a week in places like Charter Hill, Kabuki, and Little Europe; once a month in the more "upscale" combat zones like South Night City and parts of Little China.
Everywhere else, haul it yourself. Many facilities, including most corporate headquarters, contract with Coastal and SMKP Hauling separately. Assume all waste disposal specialists are armed.

#### CitiNet

Night City’s CitiNet is owned and operated by Ziggurat.
It was the first to be constructed in North America after the DataKrash and is still used as a testing ground for new technology. As a result, it is probably the fastest CitiNet on the continent, but it is also subject to slowdowns and outages as the system is revamped or upgraded. That’s on top of service interruptions due to construction, violence, flooding, and normal wear and tear.

#### Shopping

While the classic American "megamall" died with the 4th Corporate War, Night City still provides plenty of shopping options. A reduction in corporate power during the Time of the Red has allowed smaller, specialty stores to flourish, so if you’re hunting for something unique or niche, search this book for a listing.
When it comes to the food and sundries needed for daily life, most Night City residents buy out of vendits or shop at local bodegas or chain stores like Oasis (owned by Continental Brands) and Hundred Under Haven (owned by Petrochem). Branches of both corp-owned stores can be found in almost every district in Night City — they are so ubiquitous, in fact, we don’t list them in this guide unless there’s something unique or special about the location.If you’re looking for something illegal, unique, expensive, or free of taxation, make friends with your local Fixer so they can slip you an invitation to their Night Market, a "pop-up" space that’s half shopping experience, half community event. If you’re affluent, you’ll receive an invitation to one of Night City’s Midnight Markets, where the elite rub elbows and buy luxury goods beyond the dreams of the average streetrat.

### Night City Media

Media is everywhere in Night City. Playing at your home, pinging away on your Agent, blasting through store speakers, and broadcasting from billboards, the sides of buildings, and blimps. Finding a moment of true quiet inside the city is next to impossible, so learn to live with it.

#### Microstations

Microradio stations, running with just enough juice to reach a few city blocks, are especially popular among community groups and small organizations looking to share a specific viewpoint or programming.

#### Pirate Stations

Pirate broadcast stations are common in Night City. Some last only a few hours, while others have been on the air for months, pumping out whatever content they feel like sharing with the world – from political messages to pirated media.
Occasionally, a pirate media group hijacks a larger broadcaster’s signal. Sometimes the pirates get away with the interruption for a few hours and escape, but usually their broadcast ends in a hail of gunfire as corporate-hired mercenaries take back the airwaves using perfectly-legal violence.

#### Screamsheets

Newspapers are dead. In their place, we have screamsheets: custom-generated periodicals containing only the content you want to read, selected either through your own choices or by your Agent, based on your preferences.
Each screamsheet outlet produces its own content, but also "borrows" from a variety of sources, ranging from other periodicals to book excerpts and blog posts. The legality of this "borrowing" is a matter of intense debate, but so far the government doesn’t seem interested in resolving the issue through legislation. You can use a Data Term to print screamsheets onto plastifax pages (1eb per page), but most people in Night City read them on an Agent.
Night City’s screamsheet landscape is dominated by two outlets.
Night City Today News is a more formal, traditional screamsheet that specializes in breaking news. Some consider it dry and old-fashioned, but it appeals to those looking for "authoritative" content.
The Augmented Optic reads more like a collection of informal but informative blog posts, which appeals to younger readers.

#### The Data Pool

We use the terms CitiNet and Data Pool interchangeably, but there is technically a difference between the two.
CitiNets are the physical networks – the actual wires and machines. The Data Pool is the collection of information and media shared on a CitiNet. That’s where we find PopMedia.
Originally entertainment and informative content produced by independent sources, PopMedia has evolved over the past decade to include anything you can experience on the Data Pool. That includes mediacorp programming, as all major outlets (Net54 and WNS/DMS included) have been forced to adopt the Data Pool as a way to reach their audience due to its popularity.
The main driver of content on the Data Pool is Ziggurat's The Garden, where anyone with an account can set up a "Garden Patch" and share their creations.
