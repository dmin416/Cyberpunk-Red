New Westbrook
The 4th Corporate War transformed the area from a 
beaverville paradise into a giant refugee camp for more 
than a generation. Now, decades later, the pressure is 
starting to ease up. Not sure that’s a good thing. The 
corporations have moved in and taken over, setting 
up screens everywhere to advertise their products. The 
screens flicker constantly, making the night almost as 
bright as the day. You either get used to the glow or you 
don’t sleep. Not that many people can sleep comfortably 
in tent cities, cramped cube hotels, and rusting cargo con -
tainers, anyway.
City ManagerCity Manager
Meet Belkis Abera  (Exec 6 ). She’s a classic "street 
corner to corner office" story, and we know that 
because her official Network 54-produced biography 
tells us so. News profiles spotlight Ms. Abera’s pride in 
her Ethiopian heritage and her tireless advocacy work 
on behalf of the people of New Westbrook during the 
2030s and early 2040s.
Maybe that’s true, but I haven’t found a scrap of cor -
roborating evidence to suggest Ms. Abera even lived 
in New Westbrook in the 2030s, much less spent time 
advocating for it. What I have found are rumors suggest -
ing she’s been placed in the role of City Manager by her 
corporate sponsors, Network 54, WorldSat, and Rocklin 
Augmentics. I don’t have enough to run the story yet, but 
once I find the smoking gun that proves Belkis Abera 
stepped into the City Manager’s office direct from central 
casting, I’ll broadcast it to the world.
PopulationPopulation
Thousands call New Westbrook home, but no one’s run 
a census to determine the exact number . The "official" 
population  – citizens  with SINs – makes up just a f rac-
tion of that number . Most New Westbrook residents lost 
their legal identity due to the DataKrash or were born 
here without one. As far as Night City is concerned, they 
don’t exist.
For most people living here, l ife in New Westbrook  is 
about finding and holding on to whatever space you can. 
Many  of the fancy houses from before the 4 th Corporate 
War were broken up into conapts long ago . What used to be someone’s walk-in closet is now a n entire apart -
ment . The strip malls and big -box stores have transformed 
into cube  hotels  with parking lots full of cargo container 
housing. Even the empty swimming pools and cinder 
block-walled  backyards  have been roofed over and 
turned into "pool-pads" and "cinder-cubes." The nice ones 
are covered with scavenged solar panels, while the worst 
are lucky to be draped in plastic sheeting .
The fraction of the population with SINs and decent-pay -
ing jobs likes to play corpzoner. They live in a different New 
Westbrook, with locking doors and affordable food. When 
they walk or drive through the district, they look past the 
SINless masses, pretending they don’t exist. It isn’t hard to 
ignore the truth. All they need to do is look at the screens.
Security ProviderSecurity Provider
New Westbrook remains under the direct watch of the  
Night City Police Department. When the first refugees from 
the 4CW poured into the district, a state of emergency  
was declared. Technically, that hasn’t been lifted, s o the 
NCPD is used to sweeping powers when it comes to 
policing the district.
In New Westbrook, NCPD’s mandate is "keeping the 
peace." That generally means keeping the district quiet and 
somewhat safe for the official residents. The cops are quick 
to put down riots – and large public gatherings in general 
– and patrol in heavy vehicles armed with turrets. Spraying 
down the SINless with rubber bullets is a favorite pastime. 
Recent HistoryRecent History
What has kept New Westbrook from sliding into as sad 
a state as some other outlying parts of Night City is the 
advantage of the high groun d. Literally. The Westbrook 
Hills overlook the rest of Night City , making them prime 
territory for transmissions, both visual and otherwise. That’s 
why both Network 54 and WorldSat set up shop up here, 
and they have a vested interest in keeping the district at 
least intact and reasonably quiet.
Belkis Abera recently rubber-stamped approval for 
Megabuilding H6. NCPD swooped in and cleared out three 
homeless camps to make room for the construction project. 
Thirty-seven people died during the "clean-up action."

#1 Aerocab
Security Code 6 : 6th Street, Hardened Light ◊  Utility Code : 4
Night City’s original purveyor of aerial-based hired 
transport, Aerocab, keeps and maintains a fleet of fifteen 
AVs in a series of hangars set into the upper floors of this 
well-fortified building. The lower floors serve as a man -
agement center and dispatch hub. Aerocab positions 
itself as the luxurious choice for transportation in Night 
City, and it shows in the crisp, clean uniforms worn by 
the drivers and the gorgeous shine on every flying taxi in 
the fleet. Security for Aerocab’s headquarters is provided, 
under contract, by 6th Street.
#2 Augmented Optic
Security Code 5 : NCPD, Hardened Light
Always the bridesmaid and never the bride. The 
Augmented Optic is forever Night City’s second most 
popular screamsheet. The Optic’s owner, D Daniel 
Davidson  (Exec 5 ), never misses an opportunity to 
save an eb, and it shows, from the cheap, crammed 
building that serves as the Optic’s home, to the legion 
of freelance Medias he underpays to write articles. To 
avoid transforming any of his writers into a beloved 
journalism superstar, Davidson insists all writers use the 
pseudonym "Lana Smithee" when publishing. That way, 
no one can demand more money because their byline 
brings in readers.
Despite my complaints, I’ll admit there is one advan -
tage to writing for the Optic: Davidson doesn’t provide 
much in the way of editorial oversight over content the 
screamsheet publishes. Independent Medias, like myself, sometimes use the Augmented Optic to push out our story 
to a wider audience than we could achieve through our 
Garden Patch alone.
#3 Canalside Plaza
Security Code 5 : NCPD, Hardened Light
This strip mall on the southern edge of the district is home to 
the Sizzle Jams Talent Agency, an up-and-coming talent-man -
agement company representing acts in Night City. The agency 
is named for owner and sole agent Sizzle Jams  (Exec 2 ), 
who regularly trawls Night City’s clubs in search of new talent 
to sign up. Sizzle Jam’s current top act is the Digital Divas, a 
band on the rise in the local music scene. She also runs the 
Boomtown stable, organizing and equipping livecasters and 
Garden Patch creators in exchange for a cut of their take.
The plaza also features Rick Shaw’s Taxi Service , a 
Capitán Caliente  restaurant, Hardware Value , The 
Mane Event  hair and nail salon, an Oasis , Takayuki 
Law , and the Armory Pawn Shop .
#4 Chatelaine’s
Security Code 4 : NCPD, Light
Willkommen, bienvenue , Chatelaine’s is an early twen -
tieth-century  style cabaret and burlesque nightclub, 
with staff in (and out of) period costumes  that match the 
decor: the  frames of the big wall-mirrors are gilt to look 
like brass, the mirrors the mselves are suitably scuffed and 
scratched, and artificial wood floorboards have been 
distress-painted to look like the real thing.
This place has long been one of Night City’s most 
popular queer bars . So popular, in fact, many nights 
there are more straight tourists in the audience and on 
the dance floor than anyone else. That has led to accusa -
tions that owner Lulu deLuz  (Rockerboy 5 ) has sold out, 
despite her charitable work  within the queer community. 
Lulu is known for letting homeless queer kids crash in the 
stockrooms , and for donating generously to queer causes, 
particularly in New Westbrook. Despite this , the Street 
Queens have taken it upon themselves to boycott the 
bar’s Saturday Drag Nights and declare Chatelaine’s the 
"former" scene , taking their business to Woody’s.

#5 The Combat Zone
Security Code 10 : Net54 Security, Medium ◊  Utility Code : 3
A large section of New Westbrook is separated from the 
rest of the district by a high wall. Inside lies one of the largest 
permanent movie sets ever constructed: an entire fake urban 
wasteland owned and operated by Network 54 and used 
to film shows like Combat Cabb the Next Generation , Hot 
Zone Divers , and most especially The Combat Zone .
In case you’ve been living under a rock for the last five 
years, The Combat Zone  is Network 54’s highest-rated 
show. Advertisements for it are everywhere, and clips 
from the show are shared on The Garden more often than 
recipes for a halfway-edible SCOP dog. Every six months, 
the show’s producers select fifty contestants from a pool 
of roughly five hundred applicants. Those contestants are 
then stripped of all clothing but their underwear, sedated, 
and fitted with a special neural implant. 
When they wake up, they’re alone somewhere in the fake 
combat zone where they must scramble to find clothing, 
armor, weapons, and supplies. For the next three days, the 
fifty contestants engage in a battle royale in an attempt to 
be the last person standing. To make matters worse, on day 
two, the producers activate the MIST, a shrinking circle of 
death. Remember that special neural implant? It directly zaps 
the brains of the contestants caught outside the MIST, causing 
intense pain, damage, and eventually death. By the end of 
the third day, the MIST shrinks to such a small area that any 
remaining contestants cannot hide from one another and must 
kill or be killed The contestant who survives to the end wins a 
year’s worth of chicken dinners, courtesy of one of the show’s 
sponsors, Continental Brands. 
Cameras and drones positioned throughout the area 
capture every minute, which is then cut, edited, reassem -
bled, and interspersed with interviews potential contestants 
record while applying to be on the show. Voice-overs from famed Network 54 celebrities add pathos, comedy, and 
context to each season, which runs for seventeen weeks. 
The winner becomes an instant celebrity, a lifestyle they 
are rarely ready for. Most die within weeks of the show 
airing, usually from suicide, violence, or drug overdose.
The Combat Zone is heavily guarded by Network 54 
security, drones, and automated turrets. MAX-TAC has 
been hired to perform frequent flyovers when the area 
is not in use and is authorized to snipe any trespassers. 
Additional guard units are assigned to the tunnels beneath 
the area during filming in order to prevent cheating.
#6 Denya Jinja
Security Code 9 : Tombstone Preservers/Tyger Claws, Hardened Light
While it may be a tourist attraction, this Shinto shrine is no 
scaled-down effort laden with tchotchkes for sale in the gift 
shop. Denya Jinja is dedicated to the kami of chrome, night, 
and electricity, and it serves as a place where believers 
engage in spiritual practice and visitors learn about Japanese 
culture honestly and respectfully. 
Visitors who disrupt the sanctity of the Denya Jinja risk the 
wrath of the Tombstone Preservers or, if truly unlucky, the 
Tyger Claws. Both gangs maintain a presence at the shrine, 
and members have been known to follow impolite visitors 
down into New Westbrook, where no one blinks an eye at 
a spot of violence.
Once you pass through the shrine’s torii gate, the rage 
of the city outside fades away, and you enter a calm 
space. There’s no requirement for casual tourists to partici -
pate in the purification rituals, but signage and monks are 
on hand to guide those who wish to participate. Inside, 
visitors can make offerings, draw fortunes, purchase 
charms, and watch cultural performances. A regular 
shuttle service, operated by Tyger Cabs, runs between 
the shrine and the Ceremonial Hall in Kabuki.

#8 Evergreen Apartments
Security Code 3 : NCPD, Hardened Light
Seven years ago, a slumlord named Sam Banks sent an 
armed squad into an old warehouse and kicked out the 
people squatting there. He then converted it into "afford -

Toilet facilities and cooking spaces are communal, but

#10 Holliday Market
Security Code 3 : NCPD, Hardened Light
The parking lot of a burned-out restaurant on Salinas Street 
has been transformed into a day market where locals can 
set up tents, tables, or just rugs and blankets to display, sell, 
and trade various wares, plenty of it scavenged or picked 
up after it "fell off a truck" somewhere. 
The restaurant’s basement is still intact and includes a 
walk-in freezer. The freezer no longer serves its original 
purpose, but does function as a lockable storage vault 
utilized by the market’s more prominent merchants.
#1 1 Lincoln Lot
Security Code 3 : NCPD, Hardened Light
Not all of the old parking lots in New Westbrook are filled 
with tent towns and day markets. Some smaller ones, like

the district, including from Many Hands in Charter Hill, 
and a ride back isn’t guaranteed.

#14 Net54 88.2
Security Code 6 : Net54 Security, Hardened Medium ◊  Utility Code : 4
Broadcasting from a Net54-controlled tower up in the 
Westbrook Hills, 88.2 is an automated corporate synth-
pop music station, with blocks of songs broken up by 
on-the-half-hour news-briefs brought to you by Net54

The station’s programming is organized, mixed, and

Security Code 10 : Lazarus, Hardened Heavy ◊  Utility Code : 2
After the 4th Corporate War, Network 54 built offices 
in New Westbrook, forming what it proudly proclaims

#16 Network 54 Westbrook Private Acres

#17 Night City Firestation #1
Security Code 9 : NCPD, Hardened Medium ◊  Utility Code : 4
Night City Firestation #1 is the most functional and active 
public facility in New Westbrook. Fires are an ongoing 
danger for the district, since people here burn pretty much 
anything they can for heat and cooking. That the entire 
district hasn’t gone up in flames is a testament to the train -
ing and discipline of Station #1’s firefighters. The station 
is well-supplied with a fleet of AVs and ground vehicles 
specifically designed to fight fires. It is also home to the 
Hellstriders, a special response squad of Brimstone and 
Sheol FBCs who deploy to battle the most dangerous and 
toxic blazes, chemical spills, and industrial explosions in 
Night City.
You might be wondering why Night City pumps so much 
money into a fire station located in New Westbrook, when 
it doesn’t seem to care about the people who live there. 
The answer is self-preservation. Fire, like plague, doesn’t 
respect economic boundaries. A firestorm that begins in 
New Westbrook could spread to Charter Hill, Kabuki, 
or the Upper Marina. The firefighters of Station #1 aren’t 
servants of the people; they’re border guards, protecting the 
lives and property of the elite elsewhere in the city.
#18 North Cargo Village
Security Code 6 : Kimen-Gumi/NCPD, Hardened Light
Up near the border with Kabuki is North Cargo Village, one 
of a dozen makeshift cargo container communities  in New 
Westbrook. S et up in a n abandoned parking lot, stacks of 
cargo containers five or six levels high create a vertical maze, 
with welded catwalks and metal stairways providing access. 
The var ious container apartments are in decent shape and 
make for relatively luxurious living for those who can afford 
them. At least when compared to a tent city.
The relative proximity to Kabuki and the Upper Marina 
ensures both the Kimen-Gumi and NCPD patrols keep 
an eye on the community to ensure any trouble doesn’t

City ManagerCity Manager
Say hello to Barry "Big Deal" Delvecchio  (Fixer 6 ), a 
man with the handshake of a politician and the charm of an 
XBD dealer – if that dealer also happened to know where 
a few bodies are buried … allegedly. Barry’s got a smile 
as polished as his shiny suits, and he’s always got a story 
about how he’s "just here to serve the community," even if 
his idea of "community service" involves doing a few favors 
for the right people in exchange for considerations.
Officially, the City Manager is chosen by vote of the 
District Council – but since the greediest choombas in 
North Heywood occupy those seats, let’s just say those 
council members aren’t above letting a little financial 
incentive influence their vote. Barry knows how to play 
the game, keeping 6th Street just happy enough to avoid 
any awkward clashes, while ensuring the corpzoners get 
their power lunches and zoning variances. It’s a tightrope 
act, but Barry’s been walking it for a while, and he’s got 
the connections to back him up.
PopulationPopulation
North Heywood is Night City’s very own land of working 
stiffs. Many folks here fall firmly into the definition of "labor 
class." We’re talking low-grade corporate drones, junior 
Techs, and blue collar choombas with steady jobs on the 
Docks or in the Industrial Zone. They’re the backbone of 
Night City’s workforce, clocking in and out on the regular 
in hopes of making enough money to buy a luxury or two. 
But for every resident making ends meet, there’s another 
barely holding on, crammed into a cube hotel or rusting 
cargo container housing. You’ll also find a high con -
centration of ex-military types mixed in, especially with 
6th Street running the show in terms of security. They’re 
veterans who still carry the weight of old battles, looking 
for a little stability and a sense of belonging.
Security ProviderSecurity Provider
6th Street is North Heywood’s very own guardian gang 
– keeping the peace, but always with a price tag. These 
guys aren’t your typical badge-flashing, codebook-fol -
lowing law enforcers. Nope. 6th Street is a different breed 
altogether, born from the ashes of the 4th Corporate War, 
with more firepower than a Militech sales floor. Sure, the gang may have started with noble intentions 
– veterans tired of seeing their neighborhood overrun by 
crooks and lowlifes – but as the years rolled by, those 
ideals got a bit more ... flexible. Now, their patrols some -
times come with a side of "donation requests," and local 
businesses know that keeping the gang happy is just part 
of the cost of doing business. 
Recent HistoryRecent History
Let’s start with the ugly. In 2038, Heywood split into two 
districts: Santo Domingo and North Heywood. Ask the 
residents of North Heywood about the split, and they’ll 
insist they wanted to live in a district where they had more 
say and the nomads had less. Ask the residents of Santo 
Domingo about it, and they’ll mention that some residents 
in North Heywood seem to dislike hearing Spanish mixed 
into Streetslang.
More recently, North Heywood has seen a rise in 
attacks from both the Inquisitors and the Toecutters. Each 
gang is motivated by different philosophies, but most 
victims don’t care about why they’re getting the crap 
kicked out of them. Last year, 6th Street used the rise in 
violent crime as an excuse to push for a security contract 
from the District Council. 
The guardian gang rallied the community to their side 
with promises of protection until Big Deal Delvecchio had 
to choose: deputize 6th Street or risk a district-wide riot.

Security Code 9 : 6th Street, Hardened Medium
Big Deal Delvecchio gave AmeriCar every possible 
"incentive" to build its new regional distribution center 
in North Heywood. In fact, our beloved City Manager 
seems physically incapable of engaging in conversation 
without mentioning the distribution center as an example 
of his "dedication to the district’s growth and prosperity."
Built like a fortress of corrugated steel, the AmeriCar 
distribution center is the midwestern automotive neocorp’s 
first major extension into the Pacifica Confederation. 
Inside, hundreds of vehicles wait to be sold to dealerships 
and Fixers – everything from their classic EconoCompact, 
a three-seater that puts "no frills" back into "economy," to 
the ever-practical Family Star Van, which is roomy enough 
to fit an entire edgerunning crew.
AmeriCar pays 6th Street a tidy sum to guard its assets, 
and Aldecaldo convoys stop by several times a month to 
load and unload cars as needed. Needless to say, the 
place is well protected.
#3 The Armory
Security Code 10 : 6th Street, Hardened Medium
If you’re strolling through North Heywood and spot a 
building that looks like it’s ready for the next Corporate 
War, you’ve found 6th Street’s home base. This nine-story, 
retrofitted National Guard armory has been standing 
strong since the 1950s, and it’s still packing plenty of heat. 
After getting a facelift, courtesy of the gang, the Armory 
now boasts everything a modern militia could dream of: a 
garage for armored vehicles, weapon lockers filled to the 
brim, and a military-grade communication suite that keeps 
6th Street in touch with its members across Night City.
Step inside – assuming you’ve got permission, of course 
– and you’ll find that the lower floors are a combination 
of barracks, canteen, rec rooms, training facilities, and 
equipment and weapon storage facilities, with a common 
area where the gang kicks back after a long day of "com -
munity service." Upstairs, the big shots run the show. You’ll 
find offices for the senior 6th Street brass, complete with war maps, trophies from past ops, and enough security 
measures to make you think twice before pulling any 
stunts.
The Armory’s real crown jewel is buried beneath it: a 
massive fallout shelter, a relic from the days when the entire 
world teetered on nuclear war. Now, it’s the ultimate emer -
gency hideout, stocked and ready in case things heat up. 
#4 Blue Air Studio Apartments
Security Code 4 : 6th Street, Hardened Light
Perched right on the edge of the city, this little slice of 
real estate started life as a no-name motel painted sky 
blue. A clever owner saw opportunity in the 2030s and 
converted those cheap, rent-by-night rooms into long-
term digs.
The place has a charm all its own: a mix of old-school 
roadside motel vibes and a patched-up, lived-in feel that 
only comes from decades of Time of the Red DIY renova -
tions. Each room has the basics: a kitchenette that used to 
be a minibar, a bed that definitely came from the original 
motel days, and windows with a view of the road. And 
hey, if you’re lucky, your unit might even have a vintage 
tacky neon cactus lamp on the nightstand.
The residents here are mostly nomads – the kind with 
long-term contracts in the city who might be thinking about 
giving up the wandering life … or who just need space 
from their kin back in the Aldecaldo Camp.

#6 GunMart Manufacturing
Security Code 10 : GunMart Security, Heavy
Inside GunMart’s manufacturing plant, industrial robots 
dump machined components onto conveyor belts, then 
humans do the rest. Each worker has one specific task in 
the assembly process. As the weapons roll by, each adds 
their part before passing the piece on to the next person 
on the line. 
Two giant HVAC systems on the roof are supposed to 
keep the district’s pervasive smog from messing up the 
machines. Unfortunately, those HVAC systems break down 
about as often as they function. When they fail, the plant 
turns into a sauna, and the workers don masks and start 
propping open doors and windows to make the place 
bearable. Security pretends to intervene and close the 
windows, but the truth is they’re just as glad for the fresh 
air as everyone else … at least until a supervisor comes

the southern portion of Heywood. When the population

Charismatic, intelligent, and utterly ruthless, Theresa 
Valentino exploded onto the political stage backed by El 
Norte money and running on a simple message: a Santo 
Domingo for all people… not just the nomads. She won 
in a landslide.
PopulationPopulation
The people of Santo Domingo are resourceful, 
hard-working, and eminently practical. They know how 
to make do. That could mean building walls from old 
fiberboard that needs replacing after every rain, or it 
could mean accepting day work from corporations, 
nomads, and criminal operations alike. Expect to regu -
larly hear phrases like "Ahí resuelvo" ("I’ll make do") and 
"Ahí resolveremos. Como siempre" ("We'll find a way. 
Like always") from residents. The moral high ground is 
for people who aren’t worrying about where their next 
meal comes from.
As the district’s name suggests, the majority of residents 
are of Latin/Hispanic descent. Even those with other her -
itages pepper their Streetslang with Spanish picked up 
through osmosis. 
Security ProviderSecurity Provider

group of nomads, known as the Aldecaldo Peacekeepers, 
to serve as day-to-day law enforcement in the district. 
Thus far, Theresa Valentino hasn’t canceled the contract – 
perhaps as a way to make peace with the nomads. NCPD 
also maintains a presence in the area, but Precinct #2 is 
understaffed and overworked. The cops have taken to

Recent HistoryRecent History

at true democracy, and so what if she is allied with the 
Cartel? Yes, El Norte smuggles contraband and breaks

Camp. Or so they say.

static input/output.

#6 Ciudad Techito
Security Code 7 : Aldecaldo Peacekeepers, Hardened Light
As you traverse Santo Domingo, you might notice a

the enduring spirit of Santo Domingo’s residents.
In other districts, this might be a tent city. Here, the locals 
managed to improvise housing using whatever supplies

beaverville this area used to be. 
As you may guess, Ciudad Techito’s name comes from 
the sight of hundreds of rooftops, made from materials that

to create a mesmerizing mosaic of color and texture. 
Don’t worry too much about mapping Ciudad Techito.

changes.
