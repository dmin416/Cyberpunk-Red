Instinct drives us to gather into groups. Call them herds … packs … cliques … whatever you like … humans need to belong to something. Groups give us purpose and identity. That explains why Night City is full of factions people can build an identity around, whether they’re bodysculpting and chipping themselves to resemble an old movie star or proudly wearing a security contractor logo on their chest.
Every one of the following groups is dangerous in its own way and if you’re an outsider in Night City, it’s essential you know them. The time may come when you’re forced to confront or cooperate with one of the city’s factions, and knowing their style will get you a lot further than the new smartgun you just chipped in.
For the purposes of this guide, this chapter focuses on factions you’re more likely to run in during day to day on The Street: gangs, criminal organizations, and security firms. Honestly, in the Time of the Red, the line between those three types can be pretty damn fuzzy.
The logos presented here might change depending on how and where they are displayed. An embroidered patch on a jacket or a transfer on a t-shirt tends to look sharper and cleaner than a hastily spray painted wall tag or cheap tattoo.

## Gangs of Night City

Whether it’s a small pack of a dozen or an army of a few hundred, the gangs of Night City are as diverse as The Street itself – guardians, chromers, posers, and freaks, all thrown together in a lethal blender, mixed and sprinkled liberally with blood.
The activities and themes listed for each gang are based on available data and don’t necessarily represent a full spread of possible interests.

### 6th Street

**Leader:** Solomon "X" Wright
**Activities/Themes:** Security, mercenary work, smuggling, extortion
**Hangouts:** The Armory, The Maquiladoras, Minimallism (6th Street Station)
**Identifiers:** Gang logo on military style patches

Veterans turned vigilantes turned private security contractors.

6th Street was founded by military vets to provide protection to the people of Night City. Over the years, the gang has evolved as its founder, Solomon "X" Wright, aspires to grow it into a PMC on the order of Militech or Lazarus. His efforts have borne fruit in 2045. 6th Street is an economical option for private security compared to larger firms, so smaller businesses hire them to provide protection when NCPD can’t. The gang’s largest contract is with the City Manager of North Heywood, under which they are now deputized as law enforcement agents. 6th Street is fueling this growth by spending more money on salaries and equipment than it brings in via contracts. To make up the deficit, the gang has resorted to less "noble" fundraising methods, including protection schemes, blackmail, and selling assets seized during dubious "law enforcement" actions.

The majority of 6th Street’s members are combat-hardened veterans with experience in military tactics and warfare. Younger members are recruited from the local population but thoroughly trained by their superiors. The gang is well armed, well armored, and its base of operations – The Armory – is well fortified.

### Albino Alligators

**Leader:** Big Bruno
**Activities/Themes:** Partying, extortion, random violence
**Hangouts:** Albino Alligator Car Wash, The Henhouse
**Identifiers:** Popped shirt collars. White alligator mascot

A party gang with a surprisingly significant influence, famous for its white alligator cartoon mascot and popped shirt collars. The Albino Alligators control a defunct car wash still hooked into the city’s infrastructure, making them one of the few reliable sources of water in Rancho Coronado. As long as this holds true, the ‘Gators will remain a player in their district, despite being armed with cheap guns, cheaper armor, and whatever gear they can scrounge.

### The Andersons

**Leader:** Molly Anderson
**Activities/Themes:** Scavving, petty theft, mugging
**Hangouts:** Variable
**Identifiers:** Denim.

ANDERSONS tattoo across the knuckles of both hands The Andersons started off as a family-oriented yogang.

As a sign of loyalty, each member abandons their given surname and adopts the gang’s name in its place. Joined together mostly for survival, over time they’ve expanded into a well-organized group of scavvers, thieves, and muggers as part of a supply chain for the Night Market run by the gang’s leader, Molly.

Molly’s recent romance with Ricky Hamilton of the Piranhas suggests the Andersons won’t last much longer. It will either break apart due to a lack of leadership or be absorbed by another gang (probably the Piranhas).

### Arzin Tynon

**Leader:** Jimmy "Dequiallo" Miles
**Activities/Themes:** Smuggling, drugs, racing, extortion, random violence
**Hangouts:** City Tot Daycare, HTown, ValueMart Village
**Identifiers:** Armorjack with gang logo embroidered on the back

A minor gang before the 4th Corporate War, the Arzin Tynon discovered its niche as the conflict escalated: black market smuggling. They were tough enough to brave the NorCal highways on their motorcycles in a post-4CW world and smart enough to recognize which goods would fit into their saddle bags but still make major eb back in Night City.

The Arzin Tynon are strong, heavily armed, and mean.

They also buy hard into the concept of American biker culture as established in the 20th century. If you’re confident and capable of going toe to toe with them, the gang will respect you. They might still kill you, but they’ll do it respectfully.

### The Bozos

**Leaders:** Multiple
**Activities/Themes:** Terrorism, torture, random violence, targeted violence
**Hangouts:** Duck. Duck. Dead!, Unknown
**Identifiers:** They look like clowns, choomba

When they first appeared, the Bozos were a prankster gang living in an artist’s colony. Bodysculpted to look like circus clowns, the Bozos engaged in harmless pranks as street performance: water balloons and paint splashes.

Then things changed. A new leader, rumored to be a cyberpsycho Tech, assumed control. This "Great Bozo" directed the gang to go darker, nastier, and crueler.

Pranks went from relatively harmless to deeply psychological, designed to push people to their limits and beyond.

As nasty as the Great Bozo is, for some clowns, it still wasn’t enough. The gang has recently fractured into multiple "circuses" engaged in a clown civil war – each attempting to outdo the other with the most "clever" and "impressive" pranks. Brainwashing streetrats and transforming them into fake clown suicide bombers, detonating boxes full of highly addictive synthcoke, harassing random citizens in a slow, deliberate campaign to drive them insane … anything goes. No matter which circus wins the prank war, we all lose.

### Culper Ring

**Leader:** Unknown
**Activities/Themes:** Espionage
**Hangouts:** Fortitude South (?), NorCal Military Base
**Identifiers:** Logo etched into jewelry or tattooed on the skin

Some people hold onto the past with zealous fervor. They believe California declaring itself a Free State (and later splitting into NorCal and SoCal) was a mistake. They want a return to the "good old days." One nation, under God, indivisible, with military rule for all. That’s the goal of the Culper Ring, a secret sect of airmen, soldiers, and sailors living on the NorCal Military Base. Most of the group’s activities are covert and involve funneling intelligence on Northern California and the Pacifica Confederation to the United States government.

The exact composition of the Ring’s membership is unknown. Since this is a gang made up of military personnel, it is assumed all members are trained combatants and armed accordingly. It is likely that a few members are high up in the chain of command, since the base’s military police haven’t yet conducted a massive operation to shut the group down.

### DeadWoods

**Leader:** Isaiah "Gunslinger" Hurston
**Activities/Themes:** Extortion, gambling, sex work, security
**Hangouts:** Fort Sumter, the Molly May, Rail Yard, Union Railroad Station, The Yard
**Identifiers:** Cowboy-themed clothing

Posergang. Guardian gang. Cult of personality. The DeadWoods are all three. The gang’s founder, Isaiah "Gunslinger" Hurston, sells members on a dream — following the code of the Old West and improving Night City by guarding the cargo trains moving through it. The Snake Nation nomads and companies with cargo on the trains are happy to accept the help, since it comes at no cost to them. The DeadWoods fund their mission by raising capital through the Molly May casino in The Glen. Gang members usually escort their charges on their motorcycles or ride "shotgun" on the train’s roof. Keeping with their theme, they prefer revolvers and shotguns over other firearms.

### The Dirty Hippies

**Leader:** Canary
**Activities/Themes:** Drugs
**Hangouts:** Bread and Roses
**Identifiers:** Faded tie-dyed clothing

Guerrilla gardeners and drug dealers, the Dirty Hippies carved a niche for themselves by specializing in smokable street drugs. "Ganga," the gang’s brand name for its product, is just dried weeds sprayed with various chemicals, but many customers prefer the ritual experience of slowly inhaling their drugs over time instead of snorting or injecting them. Despite their name and theme, the gang doesn’t much care about peace and love. They travel armed, with pistols and rifles. Don’t underestimate the Dirty Hippies. This is a gang that grows and cooks drugs in the middle of Voodoo Boys territory. Its members may be mellow, but all that means is they’ll be calm as they put a bullet in your skull.

### Dragula Racers

**Leader:** Frankie Sparks
**Activities/Themes:** Racing
**Hangouts:** The Obituary
**Identifiers:** Monster-themed bodysculpting

Largely harmless, the Dragula Racers specialize in two things: monster-themed bodysculpts and hot-rod racing in their souped-up hearses. This small gang is currently dominating the street racing scene in Night City. Members are usually armed with pistols, but their vehicles are often tricked out with both machine guns and rocket launchers.

### The Enhanced

**Leader:** Unknown
**Activities/Themes:** Promoting megaviolence
**Hangouts:** Flair (?), No/ Brainer (?), GunMart (?), GunMart Manufacturing (?)
**Identifiers:** Logo etched into cyberware or branded/tattooed on the skin

A secretive cult, little is known about the Enhanced.

Investigation and research have only revealed three facts: The Enhanced believe cyberpsychosis represents the next phase of human evolution. The cult either owns or is heavily invested in GunMart. There might be a connection to the Brainiacs, a neuralware-focused gang from the 2020s, but the specifics of that connection is not known. The Enhanced rarely engage in violence themselves, preferring to encourage it in others by flooding the street with cheap guns and manipulating factions into conflict. Members prefer violent and explosive suicide over capture.

### Eurotrashers

**Leader:** Lys "Refrain" Schärer
**Activities/Themes:** Partying, mugging, random violence
**Hangouts:** Chopper’s
**Identifiers:** 1980s fashion

The Eurotrashers are a party gang with a reverent love of 1980s fashion. Members adopt vaguely European, indecipherable accents, and spend most of their time either partying or rolling drunk tourists to get enough money to party. Eurotrashers are more likely to pull a blackjack or a knife than a gun, though they’ll break out firearms when scrapping with the Undertow.

### Fixie’s Couriers

**Leader:** Fixie
**Activities/Themes:** Courier services
**Hangouts:** D.V. Rambling Rose
**Identifiers:** Reflective caps and arm bands

An anarchist collective of bicycle and motorcycle couriers, organized by a woman named Fixie, modeled on the work of her deceased grandfather who was a dabbawala in Mumbai. The couriers account for at least half of the message and small package deliveries in Night City.

Though fastidiously neutral, they aren’t pacifists. Couriers will first try to escape a bad situation using the speed and maneuverability of their bikes, but when cornered, they will draw either melee weapons or handguns to defend themselves.

### G3

**Leader:** Hazumi Ihara
**Activities/Themes:** Sharing anime, talking about anime, pirating anime, random violence
**Hangouts:** Animelocaris, 100.0 G3 Gun-Gal Station, Rokumei-kan, Sanroo Neuro-land
**Identifiers:** Anime-themed bodysculpting, J-Pop fashion

A disturbingly violent posergang centered around Japanese pop culture and anime. They’ve transformed their obsession into a business by running 100.0 G3 Gun-Gal. Many members, including gang leader Hazumi Ihara, are skirting the edge of cyberpsychosis, but their beautiful anime-inspired sculpts and custom implants make it impossible to guess which are just zealous fans and which are ready to slice you apart with a mono-katana.

### Generation Red

**Leader:** Apex
**Activities/Themes:** Hanging out
**Hangouts:** The Farnsworth Building
**Identifiers:** Gang logo patch somewhere on clothing

A surprisingly resourceful yogang operating out of the Old Combat Zone, Generation Red recently fought off an invasion of their clubhouse by the Kill Krashers. That isn’t likely to sit well, which may be why both Edgerunners Inc. and Danger Gal have sent representatives to chat with the gang about their future.

### Gold Dragons

**Leader:** Edwina "Eddy" Sing
**Activities/Themes:** Security, extortion
**Hangouts:** Garden of Earthly Delights, Guăngbō Tower, Ling Po Imports Wreckage, the Seep
**Identifiers:** Puffy gold jackets

Originally a low-level booster gang, the Gold Dragons were elevated by their patron, David Ling Po, from street-corner ruffians to deputized law enforcement in Little China. The Dragons adhere to a code of honor derived from old Hong Kong action movies with an almost posergang level of obsession. Jump boosters and grip feet are common implants, allowing for over-the-top acrobatic feats during duels of honor. SMGs are preferred when engaging in a gunfight. Rumor has it most Gold Dragons also have heavily modified cyberoptic implants, allowing Ling Po to observe his territory through their eyes.

### Inquisitors

**Leader:** Unknown
**Activities/Themes:** Targeted violence against cyberware users
**Hangouts:** Unknown
**Identifiers:** Logo etched into jewelry or tattooed on the skin, white hoodies, gas masks

The Inquisitors believe cyberware to be an abomination that eats away at the soul of humanity. They act in secret, roaming the streets of Night City at night, attacking visibly chromed individuals, and then ripping the implants right out of them. It doesn’t matter if the victim doesn’t survive the procedure – they at least die saved from the scourge of cyberware. Lone booster gangers, especially members of Maelstrom, make for especially tempting targets.

The Inquisitors operate as a secret society, using passphrases and secret handshakes to identify one another.

Members are drawn from across the social strata. They could be your boss, your neighbor, or even that cutie from your last Garden date. In the past few years, the Inquisitors have made significant inroads in recruiting and radicalizing corpzoner teenagers, who initially join to rebel against their cybernetically enhanced, ladder-climbing parents.

The cult excels at secrecy. Inquisitors rarely know more than a few others in the gang, to prevent enemies from torturing information out of them.

### Iron Sights

**Leader:** BloodGuts
**Activities/Themes:** Theft, drugs, sex work, extortion
**Hangouts:** Alice’s, Bladeware, Ionic Semiconductor Building, Neo Galaxy Cards and Comics, Tartarus
**Identifiers:** Scarification on the face. Mohawks and classic punk fashion

The gang that refuses to die! The Iron Sights have been demolished by other gangs, most notably Maelstrom, on multiple occasions over the past few decades. Somehow, they always crawl back up from the edge of annihilation, usually with the help of a patron. The gang’s current sponsor, Hornet, has not only helped the Iron Sights survive but also dominate and grow.

The Iron Sights are an old-school gang. Mohawks, classic punk fashion, and a dedication to controlling physical territory. Most modern gangs don’t give a shit if you cross through their territory while wearing the wrong colors, but the Iron Sights hold to traditional values. If you’re on their turf, you pay a fee in money or in blood.

No exceptions. Expect pistols and cheap explosives when dealing with low-level members, assault rifles and biotoxin when faced with the upper ranks.

### Julliard Guild

**Leader:** Sebastian "Shakespeare" McBride
**Activities/Themes:** Busking, performances, protection
**Hangouts:** Nightingale Theater, Night City Plaza
**Identifiers:** Logo stickers on instrument cases

The Julliard Guild controls busking in Night City with a chrome fist. If you’re playing a corner or, heaven forbid, an NCART station without permission, representatives will pay you a visit. The first time is a friendly invitation to join. The second is a reminder. The third time, they bring weapons.

The Guild claims fifteen percent of a performer’s take. In return, members receive armed protection from "aggressive patrons" and access to plum performance spots in the city, including the prize pick: Night City Plaza. It also brokers deals with the Piranhas, hiring out members to perform at some of the best parties in Night City. Members are encouraged to carry pistols and wear light armor while performing. Julliard bodyguards prefer shotguns.

### Kill Krashers

**Leader:** Carrion
**Activities/Themes:** Targeted violence, random violence
**Hangouts:** Random
**Identifiers:** Black and red painted rippers Where most modern gangs engage in violence with a purpose, the Kill Krashers love violence for violence’s sake. They’re a classic dorpher gang made up of teens and young adults with just enough money to buy weapons and drugs.

If you spot a mob of armed, high punks with blood red rippers shouting "Fuck everything!" that’s probably the Kill Krashers. They aren’t particularly dangerous in small numbers, but they’re a ravenous storm of destruction when gathered in large groups. Expect every form of weapon – they usually loot the corpses of the people they murder to add to their arsenal.

### Lightning Cats

**Leader:** Bengal Khan
**Activities/Themes:** Survival, scavving
**Hangouts:** Graceland Medical
**Identifiers:** Feline bodysculpting On cold nights, Night City residents tell tales of a group of feline-inspired gangers who entered a back alley ripperdoc clinic for a cheap, group bodysculpting job … and came out monsters.

The procedure was horrifically botched, so the offbrand bioexotics fled into the Hot Zone to live in the shadows.

The truth is, there is a gang of cat-themed exotics living in the wreckage of the Old Med Center, but their sculpts aren’t monstrous at all – members of the Lightning Cats look like any other cat exotic. They just prefer to "survive in the wild like true felines" instead of living like "domesticated pets subsisting on corporate-created catfood." Most Lightning Cats are skilled hand-to-hand fighters who specialize in Aikido, Judo, and cyberweaponry.

### Maelstrom

**Leader:** Warlock
**Activities/Themes:** Drugs, extortion, smuggling, random violence, targeted violence, mercenary work
**Hangouts:** Ashcroft Hotel, The Dump, The Forge, Red Cab Company, Totentanz, TravlStay CityCenter
**Identifiers:** Logo etched into cyberware Maelstrom is a combat gang with some serious history in Night City. It has been around and growing since the 2010s.

Members believe in the virtues of chrome with a zealous, almost religious fervor, and it has absorbed the remnants of a dozen other boostergangs, often defeating them in battle before inducting the survivors.

Maelstrom runs a massive drug and tech-trafficking network. To aid this, it controls packs of disposable scavvers, known as Diggers, who scavenge the Hot Zone for valuable equipment. Techs and ripperdocs are kept on the payroll as well – often against their will – to cook drugs, repair salvaged tech, and install cyberware in members.

The operation is so sophisticated, NCPD’s brass can’t help but wonder if Maelstrom has moved beyond the "gang" label and should instead be classified as an organized criminal syndicate.

Expect heavy cybernetics in all members – the more chrome, the higher someone’s status in the gang.

Weapons run the gamut, but most Maelstrom prefer big and heavy over subtle.

### Mudang Gumi

**Leader:** Unknown
**Activities/Themes:** Theft, espionage, identity theft, CitiNet scams
**Hangouts:** Dream Forest Development
**Identifiers:** Unknown

The truth is, we know less about the Mudang Gumi than any other gang in Night City. What scant information we do have suggests it is a group of thieves and Netrunners who engage in sabotage, espionage, and various cybercrimes. Motivations and goals are unknown, but some believe the gang is sponsored by Tanson and works on behalf of the Korean megacorp.

### The Muses

**Leader:** Calliope
**Activities/Themes:** Partying, roller derby
**Hangouts:** Xanadu
**Identifiers:** Togas and disco themes

Based in Woodland Park – a neighborhood in North Heywood – the Muses are not just a posergang that mixes 1980s big-hair style and ancient Grecian fashion sensibilities. They’re also a roller derby squad and part of Night City’s Wonderland League. The Muses are as well known for hosting parties in Xanadu, their arena/nightclub, as they are for their rhinestone-studded razzle-dazzle on the track. Members rarely get into fights, but when they do, expect roller derby cudgels and pistols blinged up like disco balls.

### Philharmonic Vampyres

**Leaders:** The Master & Acme
**Activities/Themes:** Theatrical productions, pranking
**Hangouts:** Night City Symphony Hall
**Identifiers:** Goth fashion, vampyres

The Philharmonic Vampyres weren’t always steeped in goth culture. Originally, they were a prankster gang. The moniker comes from a prank pulled in the late 2010s, when five members of the gang photobombed the Night City Symphony by dressing up in tuxedos, capes, and vampire fangs while flying behind the orchestra on wires during a Halloween concert. Over time, the gang evolved. Members increasingly leaned into the "vampyre" theme until the dark artistic styling became more important than the pranks. Today, the gang is split in two. The most visible faction, led by The Master, are full-on goth and focus primarily on horror-themed artistic presentations at the Night City Symphony Hall. A smaller faction, led by an NCU student known as Acme, wants to return to the days of grand pranks. Despite the split, most members of the two factions get along. The pranksters enjoy the parties thrown by the goths, and many of the goths join in on the pranking, seeing it as a thrilling form of "artistic expression." The Philharmonic Vampyres are more likely to flee than fight, but when cornered, a surprising number prove adept at martial arts skills.

### Piranhas

**Leader:** Corpse Reviver
**Activities/Themes:** Drugs, sex work, partying, gambling, running parties
**Hangouts:** The Ascension (Pacifica Parties), Club Atlantis, Playland by the Sea
**Identifiers:** Logo tattooed on skin

Once upon a time, the Piranhas were nothing more than dorphed-up partiers. That changed when the gang’s current leader, Corpse Reviver, took control. Under her guidance, the Piranhas absorbed most of Night City’s remaining party gangs, brought them together, and organized them into cells known as "shoals." She also gave the gang their mission: to become the reigning purveyors of parties in the city. When someone wants to throw a bash, the Piranhas are who they turn to – from neighborhood block parties to the slickest, wildest revels possible. Even when the gang isn’t directly running a party, chances are they’re present behind the scenes to sell drugs, sex, or connections. The membership of the Piranhas includes many of the city’s best drug cooks, dealers, DJs, light and sound crews, bouncers, and sex workers.

The Piranhas can be found anywhere there’s a party in Night City, but most reliably in Playland by the Sea, where they provide a form of protection – they keep the amusement park free from gang warfare in exchange for free rein.

Every shoal travels armed with pistols or SMGs.

Cybersnakes are common, especially among the gang’s sex workers. Larger shoals usually include at least one enforcer armed with heavier weaponry. At parties, lessthan-lethal ammunition and weapons are preferred, since dead guests can’t spend money at future events.

### The Prime-Time Players

**Leader:** Vicktor Vang
**Activities/Themes:** Extortion, sex work, blackmail, random violence
**Hangouts:** Brownstone Waterfront (WaveMo Water Taxi), CMax, North Cargo Village, Silver Pixel Cloud
**Identifiers:** Media-inspired bodysculpting and costuming

For the uninitiated, a posergang is one that follows a distinctive style based on media or celebrities. This can range from simply taking inspiration in dress and culture, to full-on bodysculpting and personality adjustments. Back in the day, there were dozens of them, each operating independently.

In the Time of the Red, they’ve formed a loose alliance under the leadership of Vietnamese action movie star Vicktor Vang (or an incredibly convincing simulation). A few posergangs, such as the Princesses of Justice or the Yellow Brick Road Gang, are independent of the Prime-Time Players, but most others – including the Kennedys, the Castaways, the Final Frontiers, and the Stallones – fall under the Prime-Time Players umbrella. Members are often armed in a thematically appropriate manner. Underestimate them at your peril. Yes, people sculpting their brains and their bodies to emulate a character from a barely remembered sitcom of yesteryear may seem laughable, but you haven’t known terror until you’re cornered, alone, in a dark alley while dozens of eerily sculpted posergangs approach from all sides, chanting in unison. "Three-hour tour! Three-hour tour!" Nightmare fuel.

### Princesses of Justice

**Leader:** Adorable
**Activities/Themes:** Protection, vigilante justice
**Hangouts:** Princessland
**Identifiers:** Costuming/ bodysculpting based on the Princesses of Justice show

The Princesses of Justice would be "just another posergang" based on an old cartoon if it weren’t for its focus on protecting the victims of domestic abuse. Their leader, a powerhouse of a woman named Adorable, takes the old show’s motifs of justice and protecting the weak seriously, and every member believes in those ideals with religious fervor. They work hard to spread the word – if you are being victimized by a "loved one," go to Princessland in the University District. There, the Princesses of Justice will shelter, protect, and nurture you … or die trying.

Members are armed with weapons similar to those seen in the show, often tech upgraded to give them "special powers." Flaming swords, electrified whips, trick arrows, sticky grenades, and more. There seems to be no end to the gang’s ingenuity when it comes to its armory.

### Rat Kings

**Leader:** Mario "Corona" Fernandez
**Activities/Themes:** Partying, theft, mugging, extortion, random violence
**Hangouts:** Megabuilding H4 (Bar of Gold)
**Identifiers:** Logo painted onto jacket

The Rat Kings are what happens when you take an entire neighborhood and stuff it into a single quasi-arcology without proper social or financial support: the kids form gangs and wreak havoc. Inside Megabuilding H4, the Rat Kings are a nuisance. They cause chaos with random acts of crime and violence, then vanish into the innards of the giant structure, hiding out when the authorities try to round them up. Outside H4, they have little presence and might as well not exist.

Everyone in the lower levels of H4 knows at least one Rat King, but none are willing to speak out or turn them in … mostly because those members are usually relatives.

Most Rat Kings are usually armed with poor-quality melee weapons or pistols.

### Reckoners

**Leader:** The Beloved Gaping Maw of the Gibbering Eye
**Activities/Themes:** Proselytizing, targeted violence
**Hangouts:** 96.1 Fever Dream, The Crater, Souls Respite, Union Chapel Building
**Identifiers:** Logo worn as a medallion around the neck Night City has seen its fair share of nihilistic doomsday cults.

They rarely last. Death in the Afternoon didn’t make it past the 4th Corporate War. MAX-TAC destroyed the Second Comming in 2029. Only the Reckoners remain, preaching about the coming "Harvest of Souls," when the "Gibbering Eye of the Void" will open, gaze upon the Earth, and obliterate the unfaithful. The cult has not only survived to the modern day, but it has thrived.

Why? First, a relative lack of violence. While members are happy to beat someone into the ground and take "donations" from their unconscious body, they’re generally willing to leave the timing of the Harvest to the Gibbering Eye instead of hurrying it along with explosives. Second, the cult’s high priest – known as the Beloved Gaping Maw of the Gibbering Eye – understands the concept of marketing.

The Reckoners offer food and shelter to runaways and the homeless. They broadcast sermons on 96.1 Fever Dream, sandwiching them between music people actually want to listen to … and they sell merch. The disturbingly cute Gibbering Eye of the Void plushie was a popular holiday gift last December.

Reckoners prefer less-than-lethal weapons, often implanted as cyberware. After all, they can’t recruit you and save your soul if you’re dead.

### Red Chrome Legion

**Leaders:** Vox and Populi
**Activities/Themes:** Extortion, mugging, theft, random violence, targeted violence.
**Hangouts:** Chrome Cross, Kid Kamp, White Side
**Identifiers:** Gas masks with glowing red lenses

The Red Chrome Legion began life as a posergang – edgy teens who enjoyed cosplaying as Nazis. As often happens, people who pretend to be assholes soon became assholes, and neo-fascist ideology grew into the core bedrock of the gang’s identity. Members of the gang talk a lot about "the Great Caesar," an almost messianic figure who will rise from their ranks to conquer Night City and establish it as a hub of "a new American Empire, free of the pollution of foreign filth and corrupt cultures." In actuality, the Red Chrome Legion is a moderately-sized boostergang, locked in what appears to be an eternal war with the Iron Sights. It is a war the gang is slowly losing, too. In the past few years, the RCL’s territory has shrunk to roughly half its original size. A recent change of leadership after a violent takeover hasn’t helped.

Like their rivals, the Iron Sights, the Red Chrome Legionnaires are armed based on rank. Expect poor quality pistols and shotguns at low levels, and assault weapons and explosives when dealing with higher-ranking members.

### Scavvers

**Leader:** Various
**Activities/Themes:** Theft, scavving
**Hangouts:** Various
**Identifiers:** Various Scavvers aren’t so much a gang as a classification.

The category encompasses a variety of small crews and groups dedicated to scouring the treacherous debris of junkyards, combat zone wreckage, and the Hot Zone for salable items such as copper, platinum, old tech, and abandoned weapons. Many die doing so, often while delving deeper into ruins in search of a big score. Some Scavver groups, like the Crawling Cobras, are independent operators. Others, like the Diggers, serve as work crews for larger gangs like Maelstrom. Armaments vary from crew to crew, but most weapons are salvaged and of poor quality.

### The Shroomers

**Leaders:** Pa Saddlefoot and May
**Activities/Themes:** Survival, farming
**Hangouts:** The Underground
**Identifiers:** Specialized cyberoptics, special tokens

In the aftermath of the 4th Corporate War, a band of amateur mycologists teamed up with a survivalist group to form the Shroomers, an underground guerrilla farming community specializing in fungiculture. They keep mostly to themselves, emerging from the depths primarily to trade their crop for supplies. Initially, the Shroomers dealt with multiple Fixers, but several years ago they made a deal to trade exclusively through Mrs. Suzuki, owner of a chain of bodegas.

Decades of underground living have forged the Shroomers into a tightly knit community with a distinct culture and specific customs. The best known of these customs among topsiders is the Wander Year, when eighteen-year-olds leave the underground sanctuary of their people and explore the world above. More often than not, the young Shroomers return to their home, sometimes with a spouse in tow to help diversify the community’s gene pool.

Decades below have also given the Shroomers an incredibly detailed knowledge of Night City’s network of tunnels, sunken buildings, and basements. No one knows the city’s underground like they do. Shroomers are surprisingly well armed and well adapted to dark, subterranean combat, as invaders looking to score a lucrative mushroom haul have learned to their peril.

### The Sinful Adams

**Leaders:** Gomez and Ophelia
**Activities/Themes:** Survival, mercenary work, sex work
**Hangouts:** The Boneyard
**Identifiers:** Goth fashion What’s the collective noun for a group of goths?

A coven?

A crypt? A misery? A murder? Whatever it is, that’s the Sinful Adams. Many consider the Adams to be a posergang, since members often bodysculpt themselves to resemble goth icons of the past, but that isn’t a requirement for membership. The gang claims no ownership of the concept of "goth" and welcomes adherents from the style’s many subcultures.

Surprisingly civic-minded, the Sinful Adams have set up shop in an old graveyard, now known as the Boneyard.

There, they’ve built a shanty town to offer refuge to a small homeless community. The Adams grow food to share with their charges, brew their own booze out of berries, and even run a small school. Every Halloween, the gang organizes a carnival, open to residents from the surrounding area.

The gang’s war leader, Ophelia, ensures each member is trained in combat. Pistols, long blades, cyberweapons, and martial arts are among the most favored options.

### Steel Vaqueros

**Leader:** Ximena Rose
**Activities/Themes:** Smuggling, survival
**Hangouts:** Eagle Rock Stadium, Silver Pixel Cloud, Minimallism (XOOX)
**Identifiers:** Gang logo embroidered on back of jacket Is a nomad pack still a nomad pack if most members stay in one place? Is a gang still a gang if many of its members roam the West Coast trading goods?

Those philosophical questions of definition apply to the Steel Vaqueros, a nomad pack unaligned with any of the Seven Nations. The core members operate caravans along the coast, trading goods and providing short-term labor. Extended family and junior members stay behind in two cities: Salinas and Night City. There, they run shops to buy and sell needed goods and hold down the fort, ensuring the caravan crews always have a safe place to rest and recharge.

As with all nomad groups, expect a variety of weapons, including well-maintained shoulder arms. Their vehicles will always be in top shape.

### The Street Queens

**Leader:** Maxime Raunch
**Activities/Themes:** Security, sex work, drugs, survival
**Hangouts:** Flowertown, Silver Pixel Cloud, Woody’s
**Identifiers:** Queer iconography worn as jewelry

An evolution of the old Black Queens gang, the Street Queens have been offering protection and safe haven to the queer community of New Westbrook for years – especially kids kicked to the curb or on the run from families, gangs, and religious groups clinging to more "old-fashioned" values. It isn’t all patronage and protection, though.

As so often happens in community-based groups, personal beefs and purity tests have led to conflict.

The Queens’ leader, Maxime Raunch, considers two of Night City’s best-known queer bars – Greta’s and Chatelaine’s – to be populated by sellouts, and openly mocks and shuns their patrons. A rival queer-oriented gang hasn’t popped up in response to the trouble, but it is only a matter of time.

The Street Queens are heavily connected to the underground club and music scene on the Mainland, and have a hand in drug and sex work in the area. This occasionally puts them in conflict with other groups, such as the Weng Fang Tong, the Voodoo Boys, and the Piranhas.

As a result, members travel armed, usually with pistols, cyberweapons, and small blades.

### The Toecutters

**Leader:** Unknown
**Activities/Themes:** Smuggling, random violence, targeted violence
**Hangouts:** Unknown
**Identifiers:** Nomad leathers splattered with blood patterns

The Toecutters would be just another Raffen Shiv pack wandering the badlands if it weren’t for two things. First, raiding groups regularly strike the east side of Night City, sometimes hitting targets deep in North Heywood and New Westbrook. Second, the Toecutters have a reputation for a unique form of torture. Specifically, members slice off the toes of their victims. Sometimes, the Toecutters eat those toes in front of the victim. If they’re feeling particularly festive, they force the victim to chow down themselves. The Toecutters prefer rifles, shotguns, and incredibly sharp blades over other weapons. Their vehicles are heavily armed and armored.

### Tombstone Preservers

**Leader:** Mister Rieper
**Activities/Themes:** Security
**Hangouts:** Boneyard, Denya Jinja, the Columbarium, Herschel’s Crematorium
**Identifiers:** Shaved heads.

Nice suits Following the 4th Corporate War, the sanctity of the dead wasn’t a priority for many people. Cemeteries represented useful, open land where refugees could pitch tents, and the graves beneath those cemeteries were a potential source of valuables. One of the first laws passed by Night City’s new City Council after its formation banned traditional burials and established the Columbarium, a place where the cremains of the dead could be interred. This didn’t stop the worst elements of Night City from desecrating the dead – graffiti, vandalism, and even violence were major issues at the new sacred site. The NCPD wasn’t able to devote the resources to protecting the Columbarium, so an assassin-for-hire known only as Mister Rieper stepped in, forming the Tombstone Preservers to protect not only this site, but all sites dedicated to preparing and honoring the dead across Night City.

Members of the Tombstone Preservers are easily identified by their bald heads and somber, well-tailored suits.

Each is personally trained by Mister Rieper and is an expert in multiple martial arts and various weapons. Every Tombstone Preserver is a Solo and should be respected in combat.

### Tyger Claws

**Leader:** Shinobu the Second
**Activities/Themes:** Security, extortion, theft, mercenary work, gambling, random violence, racing, sex work
**Hangouts:** The Bodukkan, Delphi X, Houou, Denya Jinja, Ebunike Docks, Kabuki Market, Oni-Yama Jail, Oumei-ji Temple, Sanroo Neuro-land, Savage Docs, Toranoko Gakuen, Tyger Dojo/Tora-no-Ana, Tyger Works Tower
**Identifiers:** Logo worn as jewelry, a patch, or a tattoo

The Tygers Claw served as a puppet gang for Arasaka before the Time of the Red, but the nuclear explosion in the heart of the Corporate District changed everything. When Arasaka retreated, they left the gang’s tattered remains behind. In the aftermath, renowned actor and philanthropist Shinobu Nakagawa convinced several corporations to form the Night City Co-Prosperity Sphere (NCCS) and build a new home for Night City’s Japanese diaspora. To protect the people and enforce the will of the NCCS, the Tygers Claw were reformed as the Tyger Claws.

The Tyger Claws are deeply entangled in every facet of life in Kabuki and Old Japantown. The gang not only controls crime in those districts, it also controls law enforcement through its subsidiary, the Kimen-Gumi, and influences society through operations such as clinics, a taxi company, and a martial arts studio. Members carry a variety of weapons. Katanas – especially mono-katanas – are beloved, but firearms of all types are common.

### Undertow

**Leader:** Johnny Rydell
**Activities/Themes:** Random violence, targeted violence
**Hangouts:** West Hill Church of God
**Identifiers:** 1950s greaser fashion

The Undertow is a gang of hotheads dedicated to "keeping Downtown real." In other words, fighting against the Chamber of Commerce’s takeover of the district. Only, they’re terrible at it. Their methods lack strategy, foresight, or tactical thought. They hit whatever target they think is good at the time with whatever weapons they have on hand, often while drunk on Smash. The gang has a fondness for 1950s greaser fashions and Molotov cocktails, but are happy to pull out cheap pistols when fighting with the Eurotrashers or doing a drive-by on a corporate-backed construction site.

### Voodoo Boys

**Leader:** Baron D
**Activities/Themes:** Extortion, drugs, gambling, sex work
**Hangouts:** Acorn Towers, The Island, Tumble and Dry, Seafoam, Volkodav Racetrack
**Identifiers:** "Voodoo" fashion

The Voodoo Boys began life importing drugs from Florida and the Caribbean to sell to NCU students. The gang grew quickly, until it was Night City’s largest supplier of black market substances. Then the 4th Corporate War hit.

With supply lines squeezed, the Voodoo Boys retreated to Rancho Coronado and began piecing together a new business model, one where the gang both produced and sold drugs and access to other vices. This worked for a while, but the Voodoo Boys’ market share has shrunk in recent years. The Piranhas sell better quality drugs. The Weng Fang Tong sells time with cleaner and more skilled sex workers. Multiple casinos located throughout Night City offer a better and safer gambling experience.

The gang holds on thanks to intimidation, brutal violence, and cheap prices, but its power wanes with each passing day. The death of multiple members at the hands of true believers of Voudon doesn’t help.

Despite the bones through the noses and ears, feather implants in the sculpts, and tattoos, most members of the Voodoo Boys are white and playacting at "voodoo" based on Hollywood tropes, not any actual religious belief. When trying to send a message, Voodoo Boys prefer machetes and chainsaws. Otherwise, they fall back on pistols and a small supply of shoulder arms for combat.

### Wild Things

**Leader:** Jenny Nails
**Activities/Themes:** Bloodsport, mercenary work, sex work
**Hangouts:** Megabuilding H1 1 (Iron Spirit), Redline
**Identifiers:** Gang logo scarred or branded into the chest

The Wild Things have always been all about violence.

Back in the 2020s, that violence was driven by drugs.

The Wild Things were a small gang, made up of boosters who not only survived extensive drug use but thrived on it. In the aftermath of the 4th Corporate War, though, that style didn’t fly. The gang began to flatline as members overdosed on a cheap supply cut with the poison and picked fights with mobs of desperate refugees willing to die over a scrap of food.

To survive, the Wild Things retooled. Rebranded.

They’re still all about violence, but now their megaviolent impulses are channeled into bloodsport. Each member controls the monster inside of them, unleashing it upon command to entertain crowds, make money, and prove their dominance during gladiatorial combat at Redline, the gang’s club. Occasionally, they hire out for protection, bodyguarding, and hustle work, but every Wild Thing dreams first and foremost about climbing to the top of the Redline’s leaderboard.Members of the Wild Things follow a code. They believe in weapons that are either part of the body or an extension of it. Martial arts, cyberweapons, and melee weapons are all on the table, but guns are a weakling’s tool.

### Willows

**Leader:** Ingénue
**Activities/Themes:** Therapy, mutual aid
**Hangouts:** From the Ashes
**Identifiers:**

A challenge coin bearing the group’s logo The story of the Willows begins in the 2020s, with the Willow Sisterhood – a posergang. Members bodysculpted themselves to resemble legendary beauties, trained in social and martial arts, and were then hired out to serve as arm-candy and bodyguards for the wealthy and powerful. During the 4th Corporate War, the Sisterhood signed an exclusive contract with Zetatech and undertook missions to assassinate key players in IEC, EBM, and Microtech. During one such mission, the entire Sisterhood was wiped out – all but the gang’s youngest member, Ingénue.

To prevent Ingénue from drinking herself to death due to the grief, John "The Professor" Freeman of the Forlorn Hope offered her a job. Ingénue was hired to form and lead a support group exclusively for female veterans who might feel more comfortable sharing and healing in a woman-only space. The tactic worked. Ingénue dropped the bottle and threw herself into the work, naming the group "The Willows" after her lost sisters.

The Willows meet once a month at From the Ashes – a clinic in the Old Combat Zone. Its membership includes some of the most skilled female edgerunners in Night City, including Solos Kissy and Roxxi, Queen of the Afterlife Rogue Amendiares, and the Netrunner Jenny Jett. Rumor has it that when a member of the group has a personal problem they can’t solve by themselves, the Willows gear up and tackle it together as a highly effective crew.

### Yellow Brick Road Gang

**Leader:** Dorothy
**Activities/Themes:** Drugs
**Hangouts:** Unknown
**Identifiers:** Bodysculpting and costuming themed after the Wizard of Oz.

The Yellow Brick Road Gang would be just another, unremarkable posergang if not for the gang’s leader. Dorothy, aka The Queen of Oz, is a genius drug engineer. Not only is she the cheapest source for a new street drug known as Emerald City, but she has also recently developed a rather wealthy client base, all of whom rely on her to custom-cook genetically-tailored drugs that offer all the highs with none of the nasty secondary effects. Members are usually armed based on their characters from The Wizard of Oz. Lions and Totos wield cyberweapons. The Woodsmen prefer axes, and the Scarecrows carry pistols, just like in the movie.

### The Zoners

The Zoners don’t consider themselves to be a gang. They describe themselves as a movement that speaks truth to power and advocates for the welfare and safety of South Night City residents. Initially, the group sought to protest peacefully, following official channels to obtain permission. Permission was denied by the City Manager’s office, so the Zoners protested anyway, engaging in demonstrations to draw attention to the way reconstruction money flowed into The Glen but rarely made its way to the streets of South Night City. These protests often turn violent, especially when NCPD is called in to "restore order." Zoners have armed themselves accordingly.

Weapon loadouts vary from individual to individual.

## Criminal Organizations

The line between faction types in Night City is fuzzy and growing more so every day. Is 6th Street a gang or a private security company? Is Maelstrom a gang or an organized crime outfit? Ultimately, I relied on my gut when separating the more traditional organized criminal groups from the gangs. The logos assigned to each faction were created for this guide. Criminal groups on this level usually don't tag territory the way gangs do.

### The Consortium

**Leader:** Valya
**Activities/Themes:** Gambling, sex work, extortion, smuggling, piracy, blackmail, theft
**Hangouts/Fronts:** The Amber Room, Agron’s Boxed Lunches, Mestnyy Bank, Naiad’s Embrace, Zhirafa MicroVillage
**Identifiers:** An entire language of tattoos

The Consortium is Night City’s branch of the Bratva – the Russian mob. Its activities cover the usual range of illegal enterprises, though its share of all but smuggling and piracy is small when compared to other major criminal organizations.

The Consortium’s connections to the Vyriy, a Russian nomad nation, make them a major player in the smuggling market as their ships do not rely on Thelas routes to safely cross the Pacific. The syndicate also seems to be allied with Zhirafa, though the extent of that relationship is still unknown.

The Consortium has a reputation for ruthlessness and brutality, but the truth is, it’s no nastier than any other organized criminal organization. Where it primarily differs is in method. When pulling jobs, the Consortium prefers to recruit specialists, often civilians with specific skills and knowledge, against their will. To ensure compliance, it starts with threats of violence, moves up to kidnapping of loved ones, and finally resorts to implanted bombs if the first two methods don’t work.

Consortium enforcers prefer heavy Neo-Soviet weapons, both melee and firearms. It is rare that members aren’t chromed up with at least one application of muscle and bone lace.

### Eastern Tigers Triad

**Leader:** Lu Zho
**Activities/Themes:** Smuggling, drugs, counterfeiting, piracy, theft, extortion, sex work
**Hangouts/Fronts:** Gilded Phoenix Arcade, Guns & Dolls, Jade Blossom Spa
**Identifiers:** Numbers subtly embroidered into clothing

The newest of Night City’s organized criminal operations, the Eastern Tigers Triad arrived on these shores only a few years ago. Led by the sharp and charismatic Lu Zho, the Tigers analyzed the city’s criminal landscape and found a gap to exploit. Specifically, Lu Zho employed edgerunners to steal designs from dozens of corporations, then began producing cheap copies for sale at markets up and down the West Coast. It wasn’t a new notion, but at that point, no criminal organization had managed to establish a large-scale counterfeiting operation since before the 4th Corporate War.

The Eastern Tigers Triad has few friends in Night City, but its members harbor particular animosity toward David Ling Po and the Weng Fang Tong. They consider Ling Po and the Tong to be both too decrepit and too westernized to be worthy of a dominant position in the local criminal landscape.

Triad enforcers usually arm themselves with weapons possessing a higher rate of fire. Martial arts training is common, but few of them would qualify as action movie stars.

### El Norte Cartel

**Leader:** Rubén Meza
**Activities/Themes:** Drugs, smuggling, extortion, theft, identity theft, counterfeiting
**Hangouts/Fronts:** H4 Clinic, Heywood Community Bank, Northern Light Supplies, Salón de Valentino, The Maquiladoras
**Identifiers:** An orange bandanna, tied somewhere on the body, tattoos of weapons, especially pistols and knives.

Up until this decade, Mexican cartels have had trouble spreading to Night City or further north. The nomad lock on trade routes, combined with competition from other criminal organizations, prevented them from establishing a foothold.

That changed when bribable corporate-backed shipping concerns began passing through the Panama Canal. With a route established, El Norte Cartel sent representatives and funds to Night City to expand its operations.

It appears the Cartel is playing the long game in Night City, testing to determine which money-making schemes work best. Rather than focusing efforts on one or two enterprises, the Cartel is running small pilot programs to test out the viability of drug dealing, smuggling, extortion, identity theft, and counterfeiting. Its biggest investment has been in Theresa Valentino. The Cartel financed her campaign for City Manager, ensuring she beat out the Aldecaldo-backed candidate. The alliance with Valentino may have been a mistake, though, as it seems the Cartel’s people in Night City are increasingly more loyal to her than to the bosses back home.

Cartel enforcers prefer heavier weapons – usually autofire capable – over lighter ones. Reflex and accuracy-enhancing implants are common.

### Kanzaki Family

**Leader:** Shin Kanzaki
**Activities/Themes:** Construction, embezzlement, extortion, theft, smuggling
**Hangouts/Fronts:** Delta 777, Future BuildWorks, Imported Goods 4 Cheap, Savage Docs
**Identifiers:** Elaborate, stylized tattoos. Business fashion

The 4th Corporate War wasn’t kind to the Yakuza in Night City. The decimation of Japantown destroyed its power base, and the near-total closure of shipping lanes cut it off from allies overseas. The most powerful of Night City’s clan leaders, Hichigo Kanzaki, died when his penthouse apartment was destroyed by the nuclear blast. His children, Shin and Yasume, struggled in the aftermath to rebuild. It wasn’t an easy task, especially when the NCCS put its collective weight behind the Tyger Claws.

Unable to make progress in either Old Japantown or Kabuki, the Kanzaki Family focused on Watson. In the 2030s, Shin and Yasume invested nearly all of the Kanzaki Family’s remaining resources into Future BuildWorks, a struggling construction company. The bet paid off.

Through intimidation, backroom deals, and bribery, Future BuildWorks grew into one of Night City’s most significant construction operations, even securing a contract to build Megabuilding H1 1. With Future BuildWorks as a solid base, the Kanzaki are now spreading out into other operations, nibbling away at Tyger Claws territory.

Kanzaki enforcers are trained brawlers, some of whom adopt martial arts training. Heavy melee weapons are common. With both firearms and cyberware, they prefer concealability. Makes it easier to surprise the enemy.

### Skiv Family

**Leader:** Lynn Skiv
**Activities/Themes:** Construction, embezzlement, extortion, theft, smuggling
**Hangouts/Fronts:** HF&S Construction, SK Securities
**Identifiers:** Secret handshakes and code phrases.

If you know, you know.

Following the 4th Corporate War, the mafia tried to stick to what made them successful in the 2010s and early 2020s.

Crime. Graft. Corruption. It didn’t work. The post-war era was a new world, and restoring the family business required finding a new way. Or, rather, reinventing an old one. The Skiv Family’s leader, Lynn Skiv, studied history and built a new strategy based on classic 20th-century mob tactics. Own the docks.

Own the labor. Own construction. Go "legitimate." Lynn invested the family’s money into rebuilding its old construction firm, HF&S, and founded a new business enterprise, SK Securities. She then accomplished something no mafia family head has managed to do since before the Mob Wars of the late 2000s – she got in bed with the corporations. Not just one, either, but several, securing the money and backing she needed to develop the Heywood Docks and transform it into a nomadless alternative to the Port of Night City.

Skiv enforcers learned to fight on the street and are adept brawlers. Simple melee weapons are common, but most pack heavy or very heavy pistols. Assault rifles are preferred by upper-level enforcers with a fondness for the classics.

### Weng Fang Tong

**Leader:** David Ling Po
**Activities/Themes:** Sex work, smuggling, extortion, theft, gambling, embezzlement
**Hangouts/Fronts:** The Culms, The Maquiladoras, Guăngbō Tower, Madam Yap’s
**Identifiers:** Secret hand signs

On the surface, the Weng Fang Tong appears to be the most powerful criminal operation in Night City. The syndicate’s leader, David Ling Po, directly controls Little China, a successful import/export business, the Gold Dragons, and, of course, the Tong itself. It has a presence in much of the city via sex work and gambling. Some even whisper that anyone with a degree of real power in the city owes at least one favor to Mr. Ling Po.

Dig deeper, and you spot trouble. David Ling Po is perhaps too invested in his role as City Manager of Little China, and the Weng Fang Tong is having difficulty adapting to rapid shifts in the city’s criminal landscape. The Eastern Tigers Triad is slowly carving into the Tong’s territory, determined to show Ling Po what a true Chinese syndicate is like. The Skiv Family has taken a page from Ling Po’s book and taken full control of a city district – some argue doing it more successfully than he did. Upstarts such as the Tyger Claws, Maelstrom, and even the DeadWoods are running operations that fall squarely within the Tong’s sphere of oversight and yet haven’t faced repercussions. Ling Po is an old man.

Some say it is only a matter of time before he and the Weng Fang Tong fall.

Tong enforcers are highly individualistic and often oddly quirky. One might prefer body-enhancing cyberware, another arc throwers and tasers, and a third acid and drug-based weapons.

## Security Providers

In addition to the NCPD, Night City is home to a variety of law enforcement agencies and private military/ security contract firms (PMCs) that offer services ranging from bodyguarding to on-site protection to off-the-books commando operations. Leaders and headquarters noted here are Night City-specific. Some answer to bosses in other cities.

### 6th Street

**Leader:** Solomon "X" Wright
**Headquarters:** The Armory

6th Street transitioned from guardian gang to security firm last year when it picked up the contract as North Heywood’s deputized security provider. 6th Street also provides protection to other locations in Night City, including Holy Angels Church, the First Baptist Church of Night City, Minimallism, and Aerocab.

### Aldecaldo Peacekeepers

### Corporate Security

### Gold Dragons

### Kimen-Gumi

### Lazarus
